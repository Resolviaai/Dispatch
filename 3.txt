## 1. Transcription: what has beaten Whisper

On the English Open ASR leaderboard, Whisper large-v3 is now well behind. IBM Granite Speech 4.1 2B leads at 5.33% WER, just ahead of Cohere Transcribe (March 2026). The top four open models pair Conformer encoders with LLM decoders and run much slower than CTC/TDT models like Parakeet. Canary-Qwen 2.5B needs a discrete NVIDIA GPU to run efficiently, so it's out for you.

That leaderboard is English-only, though, and you speak Hinglish. For Hindi and Hinglish the evidence is thinner:

- **Tara** (Whisper-based) reports 12.06 mean WER on the Hindi Vistaar benchmarks against 12.32 for Sarvam Saaras-v3. These are the author's own numbers.
- **HEEP-Indic** fine-tunes Qwen3-ASR 1.7B and Whisper V3 Turbo, reaching 11.9% average Hindi WER.
- **Indic Conformer 600M** gets about 12% WER on Vistaar Hindi and can run on CPU.

For running these on your AMD card, **[transcribe.cpp](https://github.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp)** is the new find. It's MIT-licensed, supports Vulkan on Windows, and covers Qwen3-ASR, Cohere Transcribe and others. Its catch is that Qwen3-ASR there has no timestamps or diarization. You'd need a separate forced-alignment step to cut clips and time captions. I believe `ctc-forced-aligner` (MMS-based) covers Hindi, but verify that.

I can't tell you which model wins on your voice, because nobody has benchmarked your audio. Run a 10-minute bake-off on your own recording with four contenders: Qwen3-ASR 0.6B/1.7B, Tara, Whisper Hindi2Hinglish, and Saaras as the paid quality ceiling. Score them on speed, correct language script, and how readable the captions are.

## 2. The two-pass design

1. **Pass A, free and local:** transcribe the whole session with the fastest decent model. Rough Hinglish is fine because it's only used to find moments and score them.
2. **Pass B, premium:** re-transcribe and force-align only the chosen clips. At most 50 clips of about 60 seconds is under an hour of audio.

For Pass B, Saaras is listed at ₹30 per hour, so that's roughly ₹25–30 for a full 50-clip batch. Even transcribing a whole 4-hour session on Saaras would be about ₹120. That's a price listed by a third-party aggregator, so confirm it on Sarvam's site. Groq's free Whisper tier allows 7,200 audio-seconds per hour and 25 MB files. It's free but sends your audio out, and Whisper's Hinglish quality is mediocre.

For clip scoring, Gemini 2.5 Flash is $0.30 per million input tokens. A 4-hour transcript costs a few cents, which is the best-value paid item in the whole system. Check Google's data-use terms for paid versus free tiers first, since this is private audio.

## 3. 16:9 into 9:16: choose a layout per clip

Three layouts, picked automatically and overridable in review:

- **Crop-follow** for you alone and stable. Caveat: a 1080p source cropped to 9:16 is only about 608 px wide before upscaling, so it will look soft. Use it only when you're close to the lens.
- **Fit** for movement or multiple people. This is the full 16:9 frame scaled into the middle of the 9:16 canvas, with a blurred copy of the clip filling the background.
- **Fit plus caption band** puts the subtitles in the empty space above or below.

The chooser uses cheap signals: face count, how much the face moves, and motion. A lightweight CPU face detector like OpenCV's YuNet should be enough (my pick, untested). Fit mode also removes the need for active-speaker detection, which is the hardest piece to get right.

## 4. Fully automatic transfer

**Syncthing-Fork** is the best fit. It's still actively released, with v2.1.0.0 added in May 2026, and it syncs directly over your home network. Caveat: it has only partial support for the Android 15+ run-condition monitor, so test it on your phone. The PC side should only ingest files that pass `ffprobe`, stay a stable size, and match their checksum. A gap of more than N minutes between segments marks the session as ended, and that triggers the final selection across the whole session.

**Open problem:** how the phone's storage gets cleaned up automatically after the PC confirms receipt. Syncthing won't do it by itself. A 10–12 GB session on a POCO C65 needs a rule, probably a tiny Android helper app later.

## 5. YouTube and Instagram

- **YouTube:** unaudited projects can only upload private. Quota is rarely the limit; the compliance audit is. The audit is free but takes weeks, and a personal tool might get rejected. Until then, the system uploads privately and you flip visibility in Studio.
- **Instagram:** this looks free for your own account. In development mode, any account with a role on the app can publish without App Review. Two sources agree and one dissents (which says App Review is needed), so test it early. You need a Business or Creator account. The limit is 100 API posts per 24 hours. One source says API Reels are capped at 90 seconds, which fits your clips. Instagram may need a public video URL, so a free Cloudflare Tunnel may be required. I believe resumable upload exists as an alternative, but verify.
- **LinkedIn:** "Share on LinkedIn" posting needs no partner approval, so it's free.
- **X:** $0.015 per post, or $0.20 if it contains a link. That's a few rupees a month but not free.
