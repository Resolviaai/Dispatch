# 1080x1920 Video Size

1080 pixels wide by 1920 tall. A 9:16 ratio. It is the size Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok all serve full screen on a phone.

If your footage was shot landscape, getting it into 1080x1920 means losing about two thirds of the frame width. Which two thirds you lose is the whole question.

OpusClip answers it by tracking the subject rather than cropping to the centre.

## Why 1080x1920 is worth getting right

### One size, three platforms

Reels, Shorts and TikTok all use 1080x1920 at 9:16. Export once at this size and the same file works everywhere without re-cropping.

### Keep the right two thirds

Going from 1920x1080 to 1080x1920 throws away most of the width. Reframe Anything decides what to keep by tracking the speaker and the action.

### Full screen, not letterboxed

Anything that is not 9:16 gets padded with bars or cropped by the platform. Exporting at 1080x1920 means your video fills the screen as shot.

## What 1080x1920 actually means

1080 pixels of width, 1920 pixels of height, which simplifies to a 9:16 ratio.

It is the vertical equivalent of 1920x1080, the standard landscape HD size. Same pixel count, rotated.

Every major short-form surface uses it: Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Facebook Reels and LinkedIn vertical video.

## When to use something else

1080x1920 is for full-screen vertical. It is not always the right call.

For an Instagram feed post, 1080x1350 at 4:5 takes more vertical space than a square without being a Reel. For YouTube standard uploads, stay at 1920x1080. For a square crop that works anywhere, 1080x1080.
