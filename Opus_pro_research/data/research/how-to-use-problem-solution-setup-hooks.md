\<h3>1. Name the Pain Point Directly\</h3>
\<p>Start by articulating the exact problem your audience faces. 'Struggling to get views on your videos?' works because it validates a frustration viewers already have.\</p>
\<h3>2. Agitate Before You Solve\</h3>
\<p>After naming the problem, briefly amplify it. Explain why it matters, what it costs them, or why common solutions don't work. This builds urgency for your solution.\</p>
\<h3>3. Promise a Clear, Specific Outcome\</h3>
\<p>Don't just hint at a solution — tell viewers exactly what they'll get. 'I'll show you the exact 3-step process that doubled my engagement' is better than 'Here's how to get more views.'\</p>
\<h3>4. Use Before/After Framing\</h3>
\<p>Show the contrast between the problem state and the solution state. 'I went from 100 views to 100K views using this one technique' paints a vivid transformation.\</p>
\<h3>5. Deliver the First Step Quickly\</h3>
\<p>After setting up the problem, give the first piece of the solution within 5-7 seconds. This rewards viewers for staying and builds trust that the full solution is worth watching.\</p>

\<h3>What is a problem/solution hook?\</h3>
\<p>A problem/solution hook opens by identifying a specific pain point, challenge, or frustration that the target audience faces, then promises a solution. This creates immediate relevance and a reason to keep watching.\</p>
\<h3>How specific should the problem be?\</h3>
\<p>The more specific, the better. 'Struggling with social media' is too broad. 'Getting less than 500 views on every TikTok you post' targets a precise pain point that resonates with a specific audience.\</p>
\<h3>Should I present the solution immediately?\</h3>
\<p>Tease the solution in the hook, but don't reveal it fully until the body of the video. 'I found the fix, and it takes less than 5 minutes' creates enough promise to keep viewers watching for the reveal.\</p>
\<h3>What if my audience doesn't realize they have the problem?\</h3>
\<p>Use an educational angle: 'You're probably making this mistake without realizing it.' This creates awareness of the problem and positions your solution as necessary.\</p>
\<h3>Do problem/solution hooks work for entertainment content?\</h3>
\<p>They can, especially for life hack, DIY, or comedy content that addresses relatable everyday frustrations. The 'problem' doesn't have to be serious — 'Can't decide what to cook tonight?' works for food content.\</p>
