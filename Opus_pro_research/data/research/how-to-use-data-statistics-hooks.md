\<h3>1. Lead With the Most Surprising Number\</h3>
\<p>Open with a statistic that defies expectations. 'Only 3% of TikTok videos go viral' or '90% of businesses fail at this' — surprising data stops the scroll.\</p>
\<h3>2. Make Statistics Personal and Relevant\</h3>
\<p>Connect abstract numbers to the viewer's life: 'The average person wastes 2 hours a day on this one habit' makes a statistic feel personally important.\</p>
\<h3>3. Cite Your Source Briefly\</h3>
\<p>A quick source reference adds credibility: 'According to a Harvard study...' or 'New data from 2026 shows...' Viewers trust data-backed claims more than opinions.\</p>
\<h3>4. Use Data to Challenge Assumptions\</h3>
\<p>The most powerful data hooks contradict what people believe. 'Despite what everyone thinks, the data shows the opposite' creates a knowledge gap viewers need to fill.\</p>

\<h3>What is a data or statistics hook?\</h3>
\<p>A data hook opens with a compelling number, statistic, or data point that surprises or informs the viewer. It works by creating a knowledge gap or challenging assumptions with hard evidence.\</p>
\<h3>Where should I find statistics for my hooks?\</h3>
\<p>Use reputable sources like industry reports, academic studies, government data, or your own original research. Always verify statistics before using them — misinformation damages credibility permanently.\</p>
\<h3>How do I make statistics feel engaging and not boring?\</h3>
\<p>Connect the number to something personal or visual. '8 billion plastic bottles' is abstract; '8 billion plastic bottles — enough to circle the Earth 40 times' creates a vivid mental image.\</p>
\<h3>Should I cite my source in the hook?\</h3>
\<p>A brief source mention adds credibility without slowing the pace. 'According to Harvard research...' or 'New 2026 data shows...' works well. Save detailed citations for your caption or description.\</p>
\<h3>What types of statistics make the strongest hooks?\</h3>
\<p>Statistics that defy expectations perform best. Numbers that confirm what people already believe don't create curiosity. Look for surprising percentages, counterintuitive trends, or little-known facts.\</p>
