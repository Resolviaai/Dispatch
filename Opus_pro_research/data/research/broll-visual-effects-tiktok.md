\<h3>Key Insight\</h3>
\<p>B-Roll is used in 6.0% of clips (733,762 total), making it the most common visual enhancement beyond captions. Transitions appear in 2.4% of clips, while outros (4.6%) and intros (1.0%) provide structural framing. Visual hooks (0.04%) are rare but impactful attention-grabbers.\</p>
\<h3>B-Roll (6.0%)\</h3>
\<p>B-Roll footage — supplementary video that cuts away from the main shot — appears in 6.0% of clips on TikTok. B-Roll adds visual variety, illustrates points, covers jump cuts, and keeps viewers engaged during narration. Common B-Roll sources include stock footage, screen recordings, product shots, and environmental footage.\</p>
\<h3>Transitions (2.4%)\</h3>
\<p>Transitions between clips or scenes appear in 2.4% of videos. Effective transitions — cuts, fades, swipes, zooms — maintain visual flow and prevent jarring jumps. The trend in short-form video favors fast, clean transitions that keep energy high without slowing pace.\</p>
\<h3>Visual Hooks (0.04%)\</h3>
\<p>Visual hooks — eye-catching visual elements in the first 1-3 seconds — appear in 0.04% of clips. While rare, visual hooks (kinetic text, flash effects, dramatic zoom-ins) are powerful scroll-stoppers that complement verbal hooks.\</p>
\<h3>Text Overlays (4.5%)\</h3>
\<p>Text overlays in 4.5% of clips display key points, statistics, and calls-to-action as persistent on-screen elements. Unlike captions (which transcribe speech), overlays add supplementary visual information.\</p>
\<h3>Intros & Outros (1.0% / 4.6%)\</h3>
\<p>Intros appear in 1.0% and outros in 4.6% of clips. Intros set context and brand the video, while outros drive actions (subscribe, follow, visit link). In short-form video, keep intros under 2 seconds and use outros as quick CTAs rather than long end cards.\</p>

\<h3>1. Use B-Roll to illustrate your narrative\</h3>
\<p>B-Roll (6.0%) is essential on TikTok for visual storytelling. Cut away to product shots, screen recordings, or stock footage to illustrate your points. TikTok's algorithm rewards videos that maintain visual variety and keep viewers watching through constant visual stimulation.\</p>
\<h3>2. Keep transitions fast and clean\</h3>
\<p>Transitions (2.4%) on TikTok should be quick — 0.2-0.5 seconds max. Snap cuts, zoom transitions, and swipe effects match TikTok's fast-paced energy. Avoid slow fades or complex wipes that kill momentum.\</p>
\<h3>3. Open with a visual hook element\</h3>
\<p>Visual hooks (0.04%) are rare, giving you a differentiation opportunity. Start with a dramatic zoom, kinetic text, flash effect, or unexpected visual to stop the scroll in the crucial first second.\</p>
\<h3>4. Layer text overlays for sound-off viewing\</h3>
\<p>Text overlays (4.5%) combined with B-Roll create a fully visual narrative that works without sound. Display key stats, questions, or statements over your B-Roll footage for maximum impact with muted viewers.\</p>
\<h3>5. Skip long intros, use quick outros as CTAs\</h3>
\<p>Intros (1.0%) should be under 1 second on TikTok — or skip entirely. Use outros (4.6%) as 2-3 second CTAs (follow, comment, share) rather than traditional end screens.\</p>

\<h3>How common is B-Roll in short-form video?\</h3>
\<p>B-Roll appears in 6.0% of the 13.5 million clips we analyzed (733,762 clips total). It is the most widely used visual enhancement feature beyond captions, used across all platforms and content genres.\</p>
\<h3>What transitions work best on TikTok?\</h3>
\<p>Transitions appear in 2.4% of clips. The best transitions depend on your platform and pacing. Fast snap cuts and zoom transitions work well on TikTok and Reels. Cross-dissolves and J-cuts are better for YouTube. The key is matching transition speed to your content's energy.\</p>
\<h3>Should I use visual hooks on TikTok?\</h3>
\<p>Visual hooks appear in only 0.04% of clips, meaning they are a strong differentiator. A dramatic opening visual — kinetic text, zoom effect, flash — can stop the scroll and boost your first-second retention rate.\</p>
\<h3>How long should intros be on TikTok?\</h3>
\<p>Intros appear in 1.0% of clips. For short-form video, keep intros under 2 seconds — or skip them entirely. Long intros cause immediate drop-off. Get to the content immediately and let your hook do the work of setting context.\</p>
