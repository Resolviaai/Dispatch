# 8 Best AI Sound Effect Tools for Video in 2026 (Compared & Ranked)

For most short-form video, OpusClip is the best AI sound effect tool when the job is placing sound effects automatically on a video that is already being clipped and captioned: its Auto SFX feature detects key moments and puts effects on the timeline, and every effect stays editable. ElevenLabs is the pick for generating a bespoke sound from a text prompt, Mirelo for generating sound for a finished edit, and Epidemic Sound, Artlist, Pixabay and Freesound for library sounds. We ranked 8 tools by the job each does best, then by price, free-tier limits and how clearly each vendor documents what it does.

## Key takeaways

- **Best for placing effects automatically on clips: OpusClip.** Auto SFX detects key moments and puts editable effects on the timeline, on the Pro and Business plans.
- **Best for a bespoke sound from a text prompt: ElevenLabs.** It returns four samples per generation, and paid plans allow commercial use.
- **Best for sound generated from a finished edit: Mirelo.** It works from an uploaded video of up to 10 minutes.
- **Best for licensed catalogs: Epidemic Sound and Artlist.** Epidemic Sound lists 250,000+ effects with channel clearance, and Artlist bundles sound effects with music and footage.
- **Best free libraries: Pixabay and Freesound.** Pixabay needs no attribution, while Freesound licenses differ per sound.
- **Check the license before the price.** ElevenLabs Free is non-commercial only, and some library plans cover non-commercial publishing only.

## How to choose

Start with the job you do most often, then match it to the tools built for it.

- **If you mainly need effects placed on short clips you are already cutting and captioning,** look for automatic placement with per-effect editing: OpusClip.
- **If you mainly need one specific sound,** look for generation from a text prompt with a duration setting: ElevenLabs.
- **If you mainly need sound built around a finished edit,** look for generation from the picture, ideally with Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve plugins: Mirelo.
- **If you publish often and need clear commercial licensing,** look for a licensed catalog with channel clearance: Epidemic Sound, Artlist.
- **If your budget is zero,** look for free libraries and read each license: Pixabay, Freesound. ElevenLabs and Mirelo also have free plans with limits.
- **If your edit is transcript-first,** look for a built-in stock library: Descript.

Two more checks. A generator or library is one more app to download from and import into, while a tool inside the editor saves that step but ties the sound to that editor. For the rest of the short-form pipeline, see [OpusClip's add sound effects to video tool](https://www.opus.pro/tools/add-sound-effects-to-video) and [its transition sound effects page](https://www.opus.pro/tools/transition-sound-effects). ## 1. OpusClip: Best for automatically placing sound effects on a video you are already clipping and captioning

*Editing an Auto SFX effect in OpusClip. Source: [OpusClip Help Center](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/auto-sfx)*

OpusClip's Auto SFX watches a video, detects key moments and places sound effects on the timeline automatically. Per the OpusClip help center, it takes under 2 minutes for a 60-second clip, and it detects transitions, impacts, UI moments, ambient sounds and more, matched to the video's content and tone. It can be opened from the Auto SFX tile in the homepage Quick Start, or from the editor by opening a clip and choosing Audio in the right sidebar, then the Auto SFX tab and Auto generate sounds. Because this sits inside the same project where OpusClip finds clips from a long video, adds animated captions and reframes to 9:16, 1:1, 4:5 or 16:9, the sound pass is one more step in the workflow rather than a separate app. See [OpusClip's AI video editor](https://www.opus.pro/ai-video-editor) for the wider editor.

The placed effects are not locked in. Each one appears as a colored block on the timeline, and clicking a block lets the volume and duration be adjusted, or the prompt edited so that one sound can be regenerated. Per the help center, each block lives on a separate track, so a block can be deleted or regenerated without touching the rest, and the original footage and audio are not changed. For a specific sound, Generate by prompt takes a short description (the help center's examples are "funny sound effect" and "deep cinematic boom") and a duration, and returns three versions to pick from. The standalone page for that is [OpusClip's AI sound effect generator](https://www.opus.pro/tools/ai-sound-effect-generator).

**Pricing:** Per the help center and the tool page, Auto SFX is available on the Pro and Business plans, and the plan table marks Free and Starter as not included. On opus.pro/pricing, Pro is $29 a month or $14.50 a month billed annually ($174), with 300 credits a month that roll over for 2 months. Free is $0 with 60 credits a month, a watermark and no editing; Starter is $15 a month with 150 credits; Business is custom. The Auto SFX help article does not say whether generating effects uses credits, so check that in the app before relying on it.

**Considerations:** The generated sounds live inside OpusClip, and the tool page says they are exported with the finished video, so a sound cannot be downloaded on its own to reuse elsewhere. The help center does not describe a way to choose which moments get effects before generation, only to edit or delete them afterward. OpusClip also describes the feature around video it is working on, so it is not a general sound library.

- **Choose it if** the job is adding sound design to short clips that are already being clipped, captioned and posted from one place, on the Pro plan.
- **Skip it if** the need is a specific sound to download and reuse across tools, or a free tier.

## 2. ElevenLabs: Best for generating a bespoke sound effect from a text prompt

*ElevenLabs' sound effect generator. Source: ElevenLabs website*

ElevenLabs turns a text description into a sound effect, and for bespoke sounds a dedicated generator is the better tool than anything bundled into an editor. Its sound effects page says a generation returns four samples within seconds, and its docs list a duration setting from 0.1 to 30 seconds, a looping option for ambient sounds and a prompt-influence control. The docs' prompt guide covers simple effects ("glass shattering on concrete"), sequences ("footsteps on gravel, then a metallic door opens") and musical elements. Its page says sounds can be downloaded in MP3 format.

**Pricing:** ElevenLabs' pricing page lists a Free plan at $0 with 10,000 credits a month and Sound Effects among the included products. Paid plans listed there are Starter at $6 a month (with a first-month promotion showing at $1 while the page was read), Creator at $22 a month ($11 for the first month), Pro at $99 and larger plans above that. The pricing FAQ puts Sound Effects at roughly 200 credits per generation, and the docs list 40 credits per second when a duration is specified. At that rate, our arithmetic puts the Free plan at about 50 generations a month and Starter at about 150.

**Considerations:** The important limit for video makers is licensing: ElevenLabs' sound effects page says paid accounts can use the sounds in commercial projects, including YouTube videos, social media and advertising, and that free users may only use the services for non-commercial purposes. The other limit is workflow. ElevenLabs generates audio, so a sound still has to be dropped onto the right frame in whatever editor holds the video.

- **Choose it if** the sound is specific (a branded sting, a monster, a particular room tone) and the edit happens elsewhere.
- **Skip it if** the goal is to have effects placed on a video automatically.

## 3. Mirelo: Best for generating sound effects and ambience from a finished video

*Generating sound for a video in Mirelo. Source: Mirelo website*

Mirelo generates sound from the video itself. Its site describes uploading an edit of up to 10 minutes (dialogue included) and getting new sound effects built around the speech, with baked-in music removed, and then optionally adding generated music. That makes it the closest rival to OpusClip's Auto SFX in spirit, since both look at the picture rather than waiting for a prompt, but Mirelo works on an uploaded video rather than inside a clipping and captioning workflow.

**Pricing:** Mirelo's pricing page lists a Free plan at 0 euros with 5,000 credits a month, 3 projects and a watermark on exports; Creator at 20 euros a month with 24,000 credits and 20 projects; Studio at 99 euros a month with 120,000 credits; and Scale at 299 euros a month. Its capacity table shows roughly 8 minutes of SFX 2.0 output a month on Free and about 40 minutes on Creator, and it notes that SFX 2.0 is at a discounted rate until December 12, 2026. Mirelo's site also lists plugins for Adobe Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve, an MCP server for use in Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor, and an API.

**Considerations:** The limitations are the watermark and project cap on Free, and the fact that the sound is generated on a finished edit, so captions, clips and posting happen elsewhere. We did not find a licensing summary on the pages we read, so commercial-use terms should be checked on Mirelo's own site before publishing.

- **Choose it if** a finished edit needs layered foley and ambience generated from the picture, especially inside Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve.
- **Skip it if** the video is still being cut into clips and captioned.

## 4. Epidemic Sound: Best for a large licensed sound effects catalog with channel clearance

*The Epidemic Sound sound effects library. Source: Epidemic Sound website*

Epidemic Sound is a subscription library rather than an AI generator. Its sound effects page lists a catalog of over 250,000 sound effects and variations across more than 700 categories, in WAV or MP3, with unlimited downloads on every plan. Its FAQ says Adobe Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve Studio plugins are included on every plan, and that sounds can be used on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Twitch and podcast platforms once the channels are connected to the account (a step the page calls safelisting, which it says keeps copyright claims off the content).

**Pricing:** Licensing is the real selling point, and it has tiers. Per Epidemic Sound's FAQ, the Creator plan covers non-commercial publishing, while commercial work, client projects and digital ads need Pro or above, and TV ads need Pro Plus or Enterprise. The pricing page offers a free trial but says there is no separate free tier; its prices were not readable in the page source we fetched, so see the pricing page for numbers. Its own FAQ is also candid that no sound effect is copyright free: a subscription gives a license cleared for the platforms the plan covers.

**Considerations:** The limitation is that sounds are found and placed by hand (search, preview, download or use the plugin), and the license runs on an active subscription, since the FAQ says publishing new content after the trial needs one.

- **Choose it if** a team publishes often, needs clear commercial licensing and wants a deep catalog.
- **Skip it if** the goal is automatic placement or a custom sound.

## 5. Artlist: Best for a stock SFX catalog bundled with music and footage

*The Artlist sound effects library. Source: Artlist website*

Artlist sells a catalog of music, sound effects, footage, templates and LUTs, alongside AI tools for video, image, voiceover and music. Its sound effects page offers filters such as ambience, foley and transitions, and its pricing FAQ points to a Music and SFX plan, the Max plan, and a dedicated Sound Effects Pro plan for people who only need sound effects. Its FAQ says AI plans work on monthly credits, with some models offering unlimited generation.

**Pricing:** Artlist's pricing page did not show a readable price for the Sound Effects Pro plan in the page source we fetched, so see the pricing page for numbers. We also did not find a sound-effect-specific generation feature on the pages we read: the AI tools it lists are video, image, voiceover and music. That makes Artlist a catalog choice for sound effects, not a generator or an automatic placer.

**Considerations:** The limitation is choice overload and plan complexity: the pricing FAQ describes several plan families, and which one covers sound effects depends on the bundle.

- **Choose it if** one subscription for sound effects, music and footage appeals more than a standalone tool.
- **Skip it if** a free option or automatic placement is the priority.

## 6. Descript: Best for editing video by transcript with a built-in stock library

*Underlord in Descript. Source: Descript website*

Descript edits video by editing its transcript, and its pricing page lists access to built-in libraries of royalty-free videos, images, GIFs, music and sound effects for use in projects. It is not an AI sound effect generator or an automatic placer, so the fit is a spoken-word edit where a few stock effects get dropped in by hand. Its plans also list Underlord (its AI co-editor), Studio Sound and Remove Filler Words.

**Pricing:** Descript's pricing page lists Free at $0 (60 media minutes a month), Hobbyist at $16 a month billed annually or $24 monthly (10 media hours, watermark-free 1080p export), Creator at $24 or $35 and Business at $50 or $65. The page notes that stock library access has a limit on the number of search results usable per search, and the Creator column lists unlimited access to the stock media library. The pricing page did not make clear which lower plans get which search limit, so check it.

**Considerations:** The limitation is that the sound effects part of Descript is a library, not a generator, and the page does not describe automatic placement.

- **Choose it if** the edit is transcript-first and a library of stock sounds is enough.
- **Skip it if** the video is mostly short clips, or the sound needs to be generated.

## 7. Pixabay: Best for free, no-attribution library sounds

*Pixabay's free sound effects library. Source: Pixabay website*

Pixabay's sound effects page lists over 130,000 free sound effects shared by its community, with popular searches such as whoosh, riser, pop, notification and glitch. Its license summary says content can be used for free, without attribution (though credit is appreciated), and can be modified or adapted into new works.

**Pricing:** Free. Pixabay lists over 130,000 sound effects, and its license summary says content can be used without attribution.

**Considerations:** The license summary also lists prohibited uses, including selling or distributing content on a standalone basis, using content with recognizable trademarks, logos or brands for commercial purposes in relation to goods and services, and using it in a misleading way. It adds that only the full Content License is legally binding. The limitations are practical: quality varies because the catalog is community-shared, there is no automatic placement, and a sound used by many videos is not distinctive.

- **Choose it if** the budget is zero and a common whoosh or pop will do.
- **Skip it if** the sound needs to be unique, or the licensing needs a paid vendor's clearance.

## 8. Freesound: Best for free community-uploaded recordings when attribution is manageable

*Browsing sounds on Freesound. Source: Freesound website*

Freesound is a collaborative database of audio snippets, samples and recordings released under Creative Commons licenses. Its FAQ says each sound carries one of three licenses: CC0 (the sound can be used with few conditions, but the user cannot claim authorship), Attribution (the original creator must be credited), and Attribution Noncommercial (credit required, and no earning money from the work). The FAQ also explains a legacy license, Sampling+, that is being removed.

**Pricing:** Free. Each sound carries one of three Creative Commons licenses, so terms differ per sound.

**Considerations:** The catch for video makers is bookkeeping. Credit lines have to be written for sounds that require attribution, and noncommercial sounds cannot be used in monetized work. Freesound's own FAQ also warns that a sound used in a YouTube video can get a copyright claim through Content ID scanning, because other users may have used the same sound unchanged in songs, and says such claims can often be disputed. Freesound is free, but it is a library of user uploads with no automatic placement.

- **Choose it if** an unusual field recording is needed and the license of each sound will be checked.
- **Skip it if** a team needs a single license that covers everything, or speed matters more than cost.

## Red flags to watch for

### Free tiers that block commercial use

ElevenLabs says free users may only use its services for non-commercial purposes, and Epidemic Sound's Creator plan covers non-commercial publishing only. **Trial test:** read the license line on the plan page and confirm it covers a monetized post before generating or downloading anything.

### Watermarks and caps

Mirelo Free adds a watermark to exports and caps projects at 3, and OpusClip Free adds a watermark and allows no editing. **Trial test:** export one finished video on the free plan and check the result.

### Features locked to higher plans

OpusClip's Auto SFX is not on Free or Starter, and Epidemic Sound's commercial and ad licensing starts at Pro. **Trial test:** find the exact feature on the plan table before you subscribe, then confirm it appears in the app on your plan.

### Attribution and claim risk

Freesound sounds can require credit, and its own FAQ warns that Content ID claims can occur. **Trial test:** read each sound's license, then upload a private test video with the sound to the platform you post to and see whether it is flagged.

### No manual override

Automatic placement is only useful if each effect can be removed, so look for per-effect volume, trimming and delete. OpusClip's help center describes all three for Auto SFX blocks. **Trial test:** generate effects on a short clip, then change one effect's volume and delete another without regenerating the rest.

### Unreadable prices

If a pricing page does not show an amount, treat it as unknown until a checkout page shows one. **Trial test:** open checkout and note the price before comparing plans.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best AI sound effects generator for video?

It depends on the job. For generating a specific sound from a text prompt, ElevenLabs is the better fit: it returns four samples per generation and lets the duration be set from 0.1 to 30 seconds. For sound generated from the picture of a finished edit, Mirelo is built for that. For sound effects added automatically on a video being clipped and captioned, OpusClip's Auto SFX is the pick, on the Pro plan.

### How to add sound effects to video automatically?

In OpusClip, open a clip in the editor, choose Audio in the right sidebar, open the Auto SFX tab and click Auto generate sounds, or start from Auto SFX on the homepage. Per the help center, it analyzes the video, places effects as blocks on the timeline and takes under 2 minutes for a 60-second clip. Each block can be adjusted for volume and duration, or its prompt edited and regenerated. Auto SFX is on the Pro and Business plans. Mirelo also generates sound from an uploaded video of up to 10 minutes.

### Is there a free AI sound effect generator?

Yes, with limits. ElevenLabs' Free plan lists 10,000 credits a month and includes Sound Effects, but allows non-commercial use only. Mirelo's Free plan lists 5,000 credits a month, 3 projects and a watermark on exports. OpusClip's Free plan does not include Auto SFX, which is on Pro and Business. For free library sounds rather than generated ones, Pixabay and Freesound cost nothing.

### Royalty free sound effects for TikTok and Reels: where to find them?

Epidemic Sound's FAQ lists TikTok and Instagram among the places its sound effects can be used once the channels are connected, with commercial use needing Pro or above. Pixabay's license summary allows free use without attribution, subject to prohibited uses. Freesound sounds are free but carry Creative Commons licenses that differ per sound. ElevenLabs allows commercial use on paid accounts. Always read the license on the specific sound, since none of these pages is legal advice.

### Can AI-generated sound effects be used commercially?

It depends on the tool and the plan. ElevenLabs says paid accounts can use its sound effects in commercial projects and that free users may only use the services non-commercially. For OpusClip, the pages we read do not state licensing terms for Auto SFX output, so check OpusClip's terms before relying on it for client work. Mirelo's pages we read did not include a licensing summary either.

### Do automatic sound effects change the original audio?

In OpusClip, no: its help center says sound effects are placed on a separate track and can be removed at any time without touching the original footage, and each block can be deleted or regenerated without affecting the rest. Mirelo's site describes removing baked-in music while keeping dialogue, so how a tool treats the original audio varies and is worth checking.

### Which OpusClip plans include Auto SFX?

Auto SFX, including generating a sound by prompt, is on the Pro and Business plans, per the OpusClip help center and tool page. Free and Starter do not include it. Pro is $29 a month, or $14.50 a month billed annually. See [OpusClip pricing](https://www.opus.pro/pricing) for the current plans.
