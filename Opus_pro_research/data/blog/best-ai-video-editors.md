# 12 Best AI Video Editors in 2026 (Compared & Ranked)

For most people editing short-form video in 2026, OpusClip is the best AI video editor for turning long recordings and raw talking-head footage into short, captioned, post-ready videos. Descript is the pick for editing by transcript, CapCut for free phone and desktop edits, DaVinci Resolve and Adobe Premiere for professional timelines, and Runway for generating new footage. We ranked 12 tools by the job each one does best, then by price, free-tier limits and how clearly each vendor documents what it does.

## Key takeaways

- **Best for long recordings and raw talking-head footage: OpusClip.** It clips, captions and reframes long videos, and its AI Producer edits raw footage in chat.
- **Best for transcript-based editing: Descript.** Cuts and reorders happen in the text.
- **Best for phone-first and quick edits: CapCut and Captions.** Both are built around short vertical video.
- **Best for professional timelines: DaVinci Resolve and Adobe Premiere.** Both are full editors with AI assistance.
- **Best for generating footage: Runway.** It is a generative platform, not a clip editor.
- **Best free starting points:** DaVinci Resolve's free version, CapCut Free, Kapwing Free (watermark), Vizard Free and OpusClip Free (watermark). Compare watermarks, export resolution and credit limits before committing.

## How to choose

Start with the job you do most often, then match it to the tools built for it.

- **Turn long recordings into short posts.** Look for highlight detection, reframing and captions together: OpusClip, Vizard.
- **Edit spoken video by editing the text.** Look for transcript editing: Descript.
- **Edit on a phone.** Look for a mobile app and templates: CapCut, Captions, OpusClip's mobile apps, Adobe's mobile app.
- **Edit in a browser with a team.** Look for shared projects and no install: VEED, Kapwing.
- **Record, then edit, interviews and podcasts.** Look for recording and editing in one place: Riverside.
- **Make caption-led short videos.** Look for caption styling and fast turnaround: Submagic, VEED.
- **Do professional timeline and color work.** Look for manual control and media management: DaVinci Resolve, Adobe Premiere.
- **Generate or transform footage from a prompt.** Look for generative video models: Runway.
- **Edit for free.** Look for useful export limits and acceptable watermarks: DaVinci Resolve's free version, CapCut Free, Kapwing Free, Vizard Free, OpusClip Free.
- **Need 4K?** Confirm which plan unlocks it: Descript Creator, Kapwing Pro, Riverside Pro, OpusClip Pro (with a 4K source) or Submagic Business.

## 1. OpusClip: Best for turning long recordings and raw talking-head footage into short, captioned, post-ready videos

*Adding a long video in OpusClip. Source: [OpusClip Help Center](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/how-to-use-clipanything)*

OpusClip takes a long video (a podcast, webinar, interview or livestream) and returns short clips, each with a Virality Score from 0 to 99 based on hook, flow and trend. It adds animated captions in 20+ languages, reframes to 9:16, 1:1, 4:5 or 16:9, and imports from YouTube, Zoom, Google Drive, Dropbox, Vimeo, Twitch, Riverside, StreamYard and Loom links. A text and timeline editor, brand templates, filler-word and silence removal, and auto-posting to YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Instagram Reels sit on the paid plans. The OpusClip help center lists an iOS app and an Android app (Android 12+, in a short list of countries, English only at launch).

For raw footage rather than long recordings, [OpusClip's AI video editor](https://www.opus.pro/ai-video-editor) includes AI Producer, a chat-based editor: drop in a video of one person talking to camera and it can cut filler words and pauses and add captions, motion graphics, b-roll, music and sound effects, with one MP4 out at up to 1080p and 30 fps. It hands the project to the Claude connector or the ChatGPT plugin, where the editing is requested in chat. Per the AI Producer page, you can start for free, the planning runs on your own Claude or ChatGPT plan, and some editing steps spend OpusClip credits. Multi-camera edits and screen recordings are not supported yet.

**Pricing:** Pricing on opus.pro/pricing: Free is $0 with 60 credits a month, 1080p clips, a watermark and no editing, and clips stop being exportable after 3 days. **Considerations:** The honest limitations: OpusClip is a clip-and-publish tool, not a replacement for a full non-linear editor, and per its help center 4K export works only on Pro and only when the source file is genuinely 4K. Bulk scheduling covers YouTube only.

- **Choose it if** the job is turning one long recording or one talking-head video into many short posts.
- **Skip it if** the job is multi-track timeline work, heavy color grading or generating footage from a prompt.

## 2. Descript: Best for editing video by editing the transcript

*Underlord in Descript. Source: Descript*

**Pricing:** Descript's pricing page lists a Free plan at $0 (60 media minutes a month, with 720p local export in the plan comparison), Hobbyist at $16 a month billed annually ($24 monthly) with 10 media hours, 400 AI credits and 1080p watermark-free export, Creator at $24 a month billed annually ($35 monthly) with 30 media hours, 800 AI credits and 4K export, and Business at $50 a month billed annually ($65 monthly) with 40 media hours and 1,500 AI credits. **Considerations:** The limitation is allowances: media hours and AI credits are both metered, so heavy generation or long recordings can hit a cap, and per Descript the AI credits cover features such as Underlord, Studio Sound, Green Screen and Eye Contact.

- **Choose it if** the starting point is a spoken recording and text-based editing feels natural.
- **Skip it if** the main need is automatic selection of the best short moments from long videos at volume.

## Red flags to watch for

### No manual override on automated edits

A tool that picks edits automatically but offers no timeline or text editor leaves no way to fix a bad cut. **Trial test:** fix one bad cut without asking the tool to redo the whole video.

### Watermarks on free exports

Riverside, Kapwing and OpusClip all list watermarks on their free plans. **Trial test:** export a finished video before deciding the free plan is enough.

### Short export or video caps

Kapwing Free caps exports at 4 minutes, Vizard Free at 10 minutes and Submagic Starter at 2 minutes per video. **Trial test:** run your normal-length recording, not a short demo clip.

### Credits that expire or meter heavily

VEED credits do not carry over, OpusClip Pro credits roll over for 2 months, and Runway's pricing page lists a one-month credit rollover on some paid plans. **Trial test:** estimate a normal month of use against the credit allowance, not a single project.

### Prices you cannot confirm

Captions' page shows iOS prices only, and CapCut's page says displayed pricing is for reference only. **Trial test:** check the price at checkout before comparing plans.

### 4K that depends on the plan or the source

Several tools gate 4K behind a higher plan, and OpusClip's 4K export works only when the source file is genuinely 4K. **Trial test:** export one file at the resolution you need and check it on the platform you post to.
