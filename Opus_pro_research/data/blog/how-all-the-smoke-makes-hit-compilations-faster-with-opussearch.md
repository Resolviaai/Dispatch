How All the Smoke makes hit compilations faster with OpusSearch

May 7, 2025

The problem

The manual grind of making compilations came at a cost

All the Smoke is a popular podcast hosted by former NBA players Matt Barnes and Stephen Jackson, with a deep archive of long-form video content spanning countless interviews and trending topics. Many of these topics show up across different episodes, making them perfect for themed compilations that drive more views and create new monetization opportunities.

However, for All the Smoke, creating compilation videos was a slow, manual grind:

Manual searching: The team had to pull transcripts one episode at a time and manually dig for relevant moments

Long editing time: It often took days to create a single compilation video

Missed moments: With so much footage to comb through, great clips often slipped through the cracks

Miss out on trends: They missed out on timely trends because they couldn’t move fast enough to repurpose past content.

Known for its unfiltered conversations and in-depth interviews with top athletes, entertainers, and cultural figures, the show has built a massive following and a deep archive of long-form content covering everything from sports to social issues.

The Solution

Streamlined compilation creation process using OpusSearch

With OpusSearch, All the Smoke completely changed how they create compilations. Instead of scrubbing through footage manually, the team now uses OpusSearch to instantly find clips based on key moments, speakers, or trending topics.

Here’s the simple workflow they followed:

Search

Look for sports and culture-related themes across years of archived content

Compile

Pull relevant clips effortlessly and export to Premiere Pro for light edits

Distribute

Publish themed compilation videos to their main YouTube channel

Grow

Reach more viewers by turning overlooked content into fresh, high-performing compilations

During March Madness, they spotted a trend on social media and decided to create a themed compilation. With OpusSearch, they quickly surfaced all relevant March Madness clips from their massive content library, refined them in Premiere Pro, and published a 3.5-hour video that would’ve taken days to produce manually.
