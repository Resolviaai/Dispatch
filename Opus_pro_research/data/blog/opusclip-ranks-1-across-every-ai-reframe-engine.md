Reframing is one of the most important quality signals in a clip. In a feed this crowded, narrative is the only thing that separates you from everything else scrolling past.

Framing decides who is on screen when something important gets said. If you crop to the listener instead of the speaker and the clip attributes a statement to the wrong person. If you crop past the chart and you have published a claim with the evidence removed.

Knowing this, we built a tool to score reframe quality and ran it against six competitor products alongside our own.


How we tested it

We ran 301 tests that benchmarked OpusClip against Klap, Vizard, Veed, Submagic, Descript and Riverside across three kinds of source footage: webinar, news and livestream.

Every run gets scored 0 to 3 on three criteria.

Criterion

Weight

What it measures

Active Speaker Focus

Information Capture

Does the composition read as intentional


Scoring

The judge compares keyframe pairs, source next to reframe, and grades each criterion against them. Every run surfaces those pairs alongside the per criterion result, a confidence rating, and a written justification.

What we do differently

OpusClip breaks the frame into pieces first, then crops, while most tools resolve a frame to a single region of interest and crop to it.

Panel detection

The tool segments the frame into its regions before it runs face detection at all. A five panel news broadcast becomes five independent regions, not just one image that happens to hold five faces.

Face crops clamped to their panel

Once the tool knows the panels, each panel bounds the faces inside it. A crop cannot bleed across a panel edge and hand you half of one participant next to half of another.

Information regions

The tool treats b roll, shared screens, and graphics as content rather than background. When the message lives in a shipping map or a slide, that region can hold the crop instead of the speaker.

Chyrons and overlays

The tool finds the source lower thirds and leaves them out rather than cropping through them. A sliced chyron is worse than no chyron, so OpusClip drops the source graphic and renders its own caption, sized to read at vertical dimensions and stay consistent across a batch.
