# 8 Best AI Motion Graphics Generators in 2026 (Compared & Ranked)

For generating motion graphics from a text prompt inside a video workflow, OpusClip is our first pick in 2026: its Motion Studio tool turns a written idea into a shot plan and a rendered motion graphic, next to the clipping, captioning and editing tools that already live in the same account. Hera is the closer fit for editable, design-led motion, Canva for quick clips inside a design suite, Jitter and LottieFiles for designer-led and UI motion, and Remotion and Rive for developers. We ranked eight tools by the job each does best, then by price, free-tier limits and how clearly each vendor documents what it does.

## Key takeaways

- **Best for motion graphics inside a video workflow: OpusClip (Motion Studio).** It plans a shot-by-shot breakdown before rendering and sits next to clipping, captions and posting.
- **Best for editable, design-led motion: Hera.** Text, timing, motion and color stay editable after generation.
- **Best for quick AI clips inside a design suite: Canva.** The video generator is for paid plans.
- **Best for designer-led and UI motion: Jitter and LottieFiles.** Jitter is a timeline editor with Figma import; LottieFiles makes lightweight Lottie animations for apps and sites.
- **Best for developers: Remotion and Rive.** Remotion is code-driven video; Rive is interactive motion for apps and games.
- **Best for generating and transforming footage: Runway.** It is footage generation, not layer-based motion design.

## How to choose

Start with the job, then match it to the tools built for it.

- **If you mainly need a rendered video for social or ads from a text prompt,** look for prompt-first generation with a preview step: OpusClip, Hera, Canva, Runway.
- **If you need to clip, caption and post alongside the motion graphics,** look for one account that covers the whole video workflow: OpusClip.
- **If the output must stay editable (swap a headline, re-time a scene),** look for editable layers or a timeline: Hera, Jitter.
- **If the target is an app or website rather than Reels or Shorts,** look for a runtime animation format: LottieFiles, Rive.
- **If you want full control and automation,** look for code-driven rendering: Remotion.
- **If you are watching credits,** look for per-second or per-clip estimates before each render: OpusClip's Motion Studio is priced per second of output, and Runway lists credits per clip, so a few seconds of video can cost very different amounts.

If the same account has to clip long videos, caption them and post, see [OpusClip's AI video editor](https://www.opus.pro/ai-video-editor) and [our video clipping tools roundup](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-video-clipping-tools).

## 1. OpusClip: Best for generating motion graphics from a text prompt inside a video workflow

*Writing a prompt and choosing a template in Motion Studio. Source: [OpusClip Help Center](https://help.opus.pro/opus-labs/motion-studio)*

OpusClip offers motion graphics through Motion Studio, a separate Opus Labs tool that opens when **Motion graphics** is chosen in the Agent Opus composer. The help center describes it as built for short, designed pieces such as logo stings, kinetic titles and concept B-roll, not narrated story videos. The flow is prompt first: describe what happens, how it should feel and how it ends, optionally attach references (up to 30 images, 10 videos and 10 audio files), start from a template (Logo animation, Logo sting, Concept B-roll or Kinetic titles), and pick the Model, Style, Length, Aspect and Audio settings. Seven built-in styles are listed (Corporate explainer, 3D Studio, Crafted Woodland, Diffuse UI, Vertical Press, Neo-pop and Paper Cutout), or a custom style reference image can be uploaded.

The part we like for this job is that Motion Studio plans before it renders. **Create shot plan** returns a beat-by-beat breakdown with timecodes, plus the style, subjects, look, camera, sound and any on-screen text it intends to use. Nothing renders until the plan is submitted, the result can be downloaded, and **Revise video** re-runs the same piece with a described change. The cost is shown on the button before each step. The same account also holds the rest of the OpusClip suite: clipping long videos with a Virality Score, animated captions, AI Reframe to 9:16, 1:1, 4:5 and 16:9, a text and timeline editor, and auto-posting to YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Instagram Reels. See [OpusClip's AI motion graphics page](https://www.opus.pro/ai-motion-graphics) for examples by type, such as explainers, infographics, logos, maps and animated text.

**Pricing:** The help center says Motion Graphics is priced separately, at 3 or 10 credits per second depending on the video model chosen. Opus Labs and OpusClip share one credit balance. On [opus.pro/pricing](https://www.opus.pro/pricing), Free is $0 with 60 credits a month, 1080p clips, a watermark and no editing; Starter is $15 a month (monthly billing only) with 150 credits; Pro is $29 a month, or $14.50 a month billed annually, with 300 credits. Whether a Free account can open Motion Studio is not documented in the sources we read, so check the Opus Labs catalog for the account in question.

**Considerations:** Motion Studio is in beta, and the help center says models, settings and prices can change, so the estimate shown beside the button is the number to trust. It makes short pieces, not narrated story videos (for those, the Agent Opus composer has separate Script to Video, up to 10 minutes on Standard and Standard+). The docs describe revising a render by prompt, not editing individual layers.

- **Choose it if** motion graphics are one step in a video workflow that also needs clips, captions and posting.
- **Skip it if** editable layers or frame-level control are the requirement.

- **Choose it if** the output must stay editable (swap a headline, re-time a scene, change a color) and the team works in a canvas.

## Red flags to watch for

### Watermarks and splash screens

OpusClip Free clips carry a watermark, Jitter's page says upgrading removes its watermark, and Rive's free exports play a splash screen. **Trial test:** export one finished piece on the free plan and check it before deciding free is enough.

### Beta pricing

OpusClip's help center says Motion Studio's models, settings and prices can change while it is in beta. **Trial test:** read the estimate beside the button before every render, not just the first.

### Credits that do not go as far as expected

Runway equates 625 monthly credits to about 52 seconds of Gen-4.5, and per-second pricing adds up on longer pieces. **Trial test:** price out a full-length piece, not a five-second sample.

### Output formats that do not fit the channel

Lottie and Rive files are for apps and sites, not for uploading to Reels or Shorts. **Trial test:** export a sample and upload it to the platform you actually post to.

### No manual override

Prompt-only tools can miss exact wording or branding. **Trial test:** prompt for a specific headline and logo, then try to fix one wrong word without re-running the whole piece.

### Rights and licensing

Canva says AI output may not be exclusive, and Remotion's free license has company-size limits. **Trial test:** read the license terms for your company size and use case before building a workflow on the tool.

It varies by pricing model. OpusClip's help center prices Motion Graphics at 3 or 10 credits per second depending on the model, Runway uses credits per generation, LottieFiles and Rive charge per user or seat, and Remotion charges per seat or per render for larger companies. Yes. OpusClip's AI motion graphics page is pitched at people who do not want to deal with After Effects, and Hera, Jitter and LottieFiles each offer a browser-based route.
