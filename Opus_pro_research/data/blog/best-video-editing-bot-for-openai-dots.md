**OpusClip is the best video editing bot for OpenAI Dots.** It turns one long video into ranked short clips with captions, vertical reframing, and platform copy, then posts or schedules them to TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, LinkedIn, and more, all from a single instruction. ## What OpenAI Dots are, in one paragraph

A Dot is a persistent AI agent inside ChatGPT. According to OpenAI, Dots run on their own cloud computer, use their own browser plus the apps you have connected, and reach more than 4,000 applications through OpenAI's plugin ecosystem. You talk to a Dot in ChatGPT on desktop, web, or mobile, or in Slack and Microsoft Teams. Dots start with built-in rules for when to act alone and when to ask for approval, and you can add custom rules that allow, block, or require approval for specific actions. At launch, the first Dot is included in ChatGPT Pro and Business Premium at no extra cost.

## OpenAI Dots video editing: why video is the perfect job for a Dot

Video repurposing is the kind of work always-on agents were built for: repetitive, time-consuming, and triggered by events you do not want to babysit. A new podcast episode lands, a webinar recording finishes, a livestream ends. Someone has to find the best moments, cut them, caption them, resize them for vertical feeds, write the posts, and schedule them. That is several hours of human work per long video, and it repeats every week.

A Dot can own that loop, but only if its video tool does the heavy lifting. A general-purpose agent is good at deciding what to do. It is not good at frame-accurate cutting, speaker tracking, or caption timing on its own. That is the job of a dedicated video editing bot.

## What a video editing agent for Dots needs to do

We judged every option against the jobs a Dot will actually be asked to run without a person watching:

- **Connects as an app, not a screen:** tools available as OpenAI Dots apps, which a Dot calls directly, are faster and far more reliable than websites it has to click through with a browser.
- **Finds the best moments by itself:** the agent should not have to watch a two-hour video to pick clips. The tool should rank moments for it.
- **Handles captions and vertical reframing:** most short-form video is watched muted and vertical, so captions and subject-tracked 9:16 framing are table stakes.
- **Edits with plain-language operations:** trim, remove filler words, change caption style, reorder sections, all without a timeline.
- **Publishes and schedules:** the loop is not finished until the clip is live, so posting to social accounts has to be part of the tool.
- **Works asynchronously:** rendering takes minutes. The tool must let the Dot submit, move on, and check back, which is exactly how Dots are designed to work.

### 2. Browser-driven web editors

Every Dot has its own browser, so in principle it can open any web-based video editor, sign in, and click through the interface. This works for simple, one-off tasks. It is slow, it breaks when a page layout changes, and it cannot judge which moments in a long video are worth clipping. Treat it as a fallback for tools that have no app connection.

**Best for:** occasional edits in a tool you already pay for that has no ChatGPT app.

### 3. FFmpeg scripts on the Dot's cloud computer

FFmpeg is the open-source engine under most video software. Because a Dot has its own computer, it can write and run FFmpeg commands to cut, concatenate, transcode, and burn in subtitles. This is precise and free, but the Dot has to know exactly where to cut, and FFmpeg has no idea which moment is interesting or where the speaker's face is. Captions need a separate transcription step, and there is no publishing.

**Best for:** technical teams that already know their cut points and need format conversion at volume.

### 4. Desktop editor handoff (Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve)

Professional editing software such as Adobe Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve runs on your own machine, not the Dot's cloud computer. A Dot can prepare the brief, transcript, and clip list, but a person still has to open the project and do the edit. This keeps full creative control and gives up the automation.

**Best for:** flagship content where every frame is hand-finished.

### 5. A human editor, briefed by your Dot

The manual baseline is still a real option. A Dot can monitor your uploads, write the brief, and send it to a freelance editor. Quality is high, turnaround is measured in days, and cost scales with every video.

**Best for:** high-budget campaigns and brand films.
