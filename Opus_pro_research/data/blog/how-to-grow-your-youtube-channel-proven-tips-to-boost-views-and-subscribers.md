## **How Does YouTube Count Views and Why It Matters for Channel Growth?**

YouTube views are pretty simple. Whenever someone intentionally plays a video on their device for at least 30 seconds, that’s considered [a view](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785?hl=en).

Playing your own video for 30 seconds or longer will count as a view too. YouTube will even count views from people who watch a video more than once.

YouTube videos that are shared or embedded on other sites are considered views as well. And [live views](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/live/) are included in the metric too.

If you’re curious about your view count as a YouTube creator, take a look at your analytics. Just note that YouTube only updates these numbers every 48 hours.

## Why do YouTube views matter?

YouTube views are important for a very specific reason. While your video view counts won’t directly influence your channel growth, other relevant metrics will. Your [watch time](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9052667?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid), for example, will determine where your content shows up in the YouTube algorithm. This is the total amount of time people have spent watching your YouTube video.

Your [average view duration (AVD)](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/youtube-analytics)—or the average amount of time viewers play your video—matters too. This is another metric that YouTube looks at to decide whether to share your content with others.

Like any website, YouTube wants to engage viewers for as long as possible. The most effective way to do this is by showing people videos they *want* to watch. So to evaluate whether your videos are “good,” YouTube will look at all of your video metrics—including watch time, AVD, and other factors.

This is what makes YouTube viewership essential. You can’t boost your video metrics without any meaningful views. In fact, the simple act of getting views will feed the algorithm (and indicate that your content is worth sharing). And with users consuming [over 1 billion hours](https://www.globalmediainsight.com/blog/youtube-users-statistics/) of YouTube videos each day, there’s plenty of viewing opportunity for everyone.

We’ll repeat that your total view count is mainly a vanity metric, and that it doesn’t inherently increase your engagement. However, psychologically speaking, people are more likely to watch videos with higher view counts. These numbers show viewers that you put out interesting content.

## **What Makes YouTube Subscribers Valuable for Channel Growth?**

YouTube subscribers are accounts that "follow" your YouTube channel by hitting the "subscribe" button. This means they want to track all your content and receive your new videos in their feeds.

Subscribers can also opt to receive notifications whenever you post new content. The act of subscribing indicates genuine interest in your content and creates a loyal audience base.

Your YouTube [subscriber count](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6051134) may seem straightforward, but several factors affect accuracy. The subscriber count on your channel may not match your YouTube analytics numbers because updates can take days to process.

Your [YouTube Studio](https://studio.youtube.com/channel/UCJPvXA_A2AvL7DjVvlrZJNA) only displays subscribers with public subscriptions. New users' subscriptions are private by default, so analytics numbers may appear lower than actual subscriber counts.

YouTube occasionally purges bot or inactive accounts from subscriber lists. Fake subscribers offer no value to your channel, and inactive accounts no longer watch your content. Losing these subscribers won't negatively impact your YouTube standing.

## Can YouTubers see their subscribers?

Creators can see a list of their most recent subscribers in their YouTube Studio dashboard. Your YouTube subscriber count will be listed there as well.

To view your recent subscribers, simply log in to YouTube Studio. From there, find the “Recent subscribers” option. You can click “See all” to expand it—or choose a specific timeframe to see how your subscriber count has evolved.

Note that while you can see your subscriber count, you won’t be able to see all of your subscribers. You will only be able to see your recent subscribers if:

- **The account’s subscriptions are public**
- **The user subscribed to your channel in the last 28 days**

Again, you won’t be able to see accounts with private subscriptions in your list. Accounts will also be hidden from your subscribers if they are suspended or have been [flagged as spam](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6051134).

## Why are YouTube subscribers important?

YouTube subscribers matter because these accounts typically value the video content you put out. They *like* to watch your YouTube content and often wait for new vide uploads. Subscribers are also important because creators need [at least 1,000 subscribers](https://www.businessinsider.com/how-many-subscribers-do-you-need-to-make-money-on-youtube) (and 4,000 watch hours) to [monetize their YouTube channel](https://www.opus.pro/blog/top-5-ways-for-video-creators-to-monetize-in-2024?*=youtube). And we all know how much creators value making money online.

Now, a high subscriber count won’t directly boost your income. That said, the video engagement your subscribers bring will enhance your ad revenue—typically in the form of more views and longer watch times. A high number of subscribers will also help you bring in sponsored content deals and sell merchandise. This will make your YouTube channel more profitable even outside the platform.

Another consideration is that a high subscriber count will strengthen your credibility as a YouTuber. Viewers that see a measurable number of subscribers by a creator’s account thumbnail will be more likely to subscribe too.

While [YouTube subscribers](https://www.opus.pro/blog/how-github-uses-opusclip?*=youtube) are definitely important, they aren’t the be-all and end-all for your channel. Cross-promotion on social media and other websites will also help you increase your YouTube engagement.

[SEO](https://www.bu.edu/prsocial/best-practices/search-engine-optimization-seo-best-practices/) can’t be overlooked for YouTubers. Optimizing your video descriptions and titles will increase your likelihood of getting [discovered](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&sjid=8076342873328576238-NC)—and give your YouTube analytics a much-needed boost.

From your video keywords to your tags, here are some SEO strategies to consider:

- **Add your target keyword to the title of each video**
- **Write detailed, keyword-dense** [**video descriptions**](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449?hl=en)
- **Use all of the main tags for your videos on YouTube**

Following these SEO tips will help your YouTube content reach the right people. Good search engine optimization will also make it abundantly clear what your videos are about. Talk about a win-win for YouTube creators!

**Upload videos on a set schedule**

There’s real value in letting your audience know when they can expect new videos from you. By committing to a specific cadence, your subscribers will learn to keep an eye out for your next upload. Just keep in mind that consistency matters more than frequency. So whether you post videos on a weekly or monthly basis, the important thing is to follow a schedule.

YouTube’s [2024 vision for creators](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-2024-vision) will be a major factor in your channel’s growth.
