## **What Makes Video Podcasts Different From Audio-Only?**

Video podcasts combine the storytelling power of traditional podcasts with visual elements, creating deeper connections with their audience. Unlike audio-only formats, video podcasts let viewers see facial expressions, demonstrations, and visual aids that enhance understanding and engagement.

The numbers tell the story. YouTube has become the preferred platform for podcast consumption, with [65% of viewers](https://podnews.net/update/youtube-new-listeners) discovering podcasts for the first time through video. Video podcasts offer flexibility that audio can't match. Listeners can choose to watch actively or play episodes in the background while multitasking.

The platform challenge is real. While audio podcasts distribute easily through RSS feeds to every major platform, video podcasts face technical limitations. Some hosting platforms support video RSS feeds, while others require manual uploads to YouTube and Spotify. Your platform choice determines whether you can automate distribution or need to upload content manually to each destination.

## **Can You Host Video Podcasts With RSS Feeds?**

The short answer? It depends on the hosting platform. Traditional RSS feeds were designed for audio files, creating unique challenges for video podcast distribution.

**Platforms with video RSS support:**

- **Podbean** offers full video RSS feeds that distribute to compatible platforms
- **Spotify for Podcasters** supports both audio and video in a single feed

**Platforms requiring manual uploads:**

- **YouTube** doesn't accept RSS feeds, but remains essential for video podcast discovery
- **Apple Podcasts** currently supports audio-only RSS feeds

This split means most video podcasters use a hybrid approach. They host on a platform with video RSS support for automatic distribution, then manually upload to YouTube for maximum reach. Some creators use services like Vid2Pod to convert YouTube videos into audio podcasts, expanding their audience across both formats.

Choose your hosting platform based on your distribution priorities. If you want maximum automation, pick a host with video RSS support. If YouTube is your primary focus, you might host directly there and use a separate service for audio distribution.

## **10 Top Platforms for Video Podcast Hosting**

The platform you pick can dramatically influence your reach, engagement, and wallet. Let's break down the video podcasting giants in the game.

### **1. YouTube**

YouTube dominates video podcast discovery, with an expected monthly user count to reach [2.85 billion in 2025](https://www.globalmediainsight.com/blog/youtube-users-statistics/). It recently launched YouTube Podcasts, making it the go-to platform for video-first creators.

- **Key features:** Free unlimited hosting, video-first format, powerful SEO via Google search, and built-in monetization through ads, memberships, and Super Chat.
- **Pros:** Reach over 2 billion users, get discovered through search, and monetize without extra setup. Visual-first creators thrive here.
- **Cons:** No RSS feed generation, meaning you’ll need a separate host for traditional audio podcast platforms. Success often hinges on consistent uploads and viewer engagement.

### **2. Spotify for Podcasters**

Spotify bridges the gap between video and audio podcasting. All creators can now upload video versions of their shows while maintaining audio RSS feeds.

- **Key features:** Free video and audio hosting, automatic RSS feed generation, Spotify-native video playback, and centralized analytics across formats.
- **Pros:** Easy video uploading alongside audio episodes, one dashboard for everything, and access to Spotify’s massive global listener base.

## **How to Distribute Your Video Podcast Across Platforms**

Video podcast distribution requires a different strategy than audio-only shows. While audio podcasts flow seamlessly through RSS feeds, video distribution often needs platform-specific approaches.

### **Automatic Distribution via Video RSS Feeds**

Platforms supporting video RSS feeds can distribute to:

- Spotify (video and audio versions)
- Amazon Music (audio extracted from video)
- Podcast apps supporting video playback

**Setup process:**

1. Upload your video to a host supporting video RSS (Podbean, Blubrry, or Spotify for Podcasters)
2. Submit your RSS feed to podcast directories
3. The platform automatically extracts audio for audio-only apps

### **Manual Uploads for YouTube and Social Media**

Some essential platforms require direct uploads:

**YouTube:**

- Upload directly through YouTube Studio
- Optimize titles and descriptions for YouTube search
- Add to a dedicated podcast playlist
- Enable YouTube Podcasts features in channel settings

**Social Media Clips:**

- Extract short clips using tools like OpusClip
- Upload to TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts
- Link back to full episodes for traffic

### **Hybrid Multi-Platform Distribution Strategy**

Most successful video podcasters use this approach:

1. Host on a platform with video RSS support (for automatic distribution)
2. Manually upload to YouTube (for discovery)
3. Create short clips for social media (for growth)
4. Maintain a separate audio feed if needed (for traditional podcast apps)

This hybrid strategy maximizes reach while minimizing manual work.

## **How to Monetize Your Video Podcast by Platform**

Your platform choice directly impacts how you can monetize your video podcast. Each platform offers different revenue opportunities and requirements.

### **Monetization Options on YouTube**

YouTube offers the most diverse revenue streams for video podcasters:

- **Ad Revenue:** Share in pre-roll, mid-roll, and post-roll ad income (requires 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours)
- **Channel Memberships:** Offer perks to paying members ($4.99/month typical)
- **Super Chat/Super Thanks:** Receive direct viewer payments during streams or on videos
- **Merchandise Shelf:** Sell products directly below your videos

### **Monetization Features on Spotify and Apple Podcasts**

**Spotify:**

- Paid subscriber-only episodes
- Early access content for supporters
- The platform handles payment processing

**Apple Podcasts Subscriptions:**

- Premium audio content (video not yet supported)
- Integrates with video hosts like Podbean for audio distribution

### **Platform-Agnostic Monetization Options**

These work regardless of your hosting choice:

- **Direct Sponsorships:** Negotiate your own deals, keep 100% of revenue
- **Patreon Integration:** Build a membership community outside platforms
- **Affiliate Marketing:** Earn commissions through tracked links

### **Revenue Potential Comparison by Platform**

- **YouTube**: Highest potential through multiple revenue streams
- **Spotify**: Growing video monetization options
- **Self-hosted**: Maximum control, but requires external monetization

Choose your platform based on your monetization priorities. YouTube excels in ad revenue, while Spotify and Podbean offer cleaner subscription models.
