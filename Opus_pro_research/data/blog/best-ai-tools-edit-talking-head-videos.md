## How to edit a talking head video with AI

Most AI-assisted talking-head workflows follow the same four steps. First, import the recording and let the tool transcribe it. Second, remove dead air: silences, filler words such as "um" and "uh", and bad takes. Third, clean the audio (noise reduction, clearer voice) and add captions. Fourth, frame the speaker for the target format (vertical for Shorts, Reels and TikTok; widescreen for YouTube) and export. For the full shooting and framing side of the craft, see our [talking-head videos guide](https://www.opus.pro/blog/talking-head-videos-guide). The tools above differ mainly in which steps they automate and whether the output is one long video or many short ones.

## Red flags to watch for

### Watermarks on free exports

Several free plans here (OpusClip, Gling, Kapwing, Riverside) add one. **Trial test:** export a finished video on the free plan before building a workflow on it.

### Credits that do not roll over

VEED states that AI credits do not carry over, while Captions says unused credits roll over up to a cap. **Trial test:** estimate a normal month of editing against the credit allowance, not a single project.

### Per-video duration caps

Submagic limits video length by plan (2 minutes on Starter, 5 on Pro), which rules out long recordings on lower tiers. **Trial test:** upload your normal-length recording, not a short demo clip.

### Prices shown for one platform

Captions' page says its displayed prices and features reflect iOS plans only. **Trial test:** check the price on the platform you will actually use before comparing plans.

### No manual override on automatic cuts

Automatic cuts should be editable afterward. **Trial test:** fix one bad cut in the timeline or transcript without asking the tool to redo the whole video.

### Cleanup behind a paywall

Filler and silence removal sits on paid tiers at several vendors. **Trial test:** run your own footage through the filler-word and silence tools on the plan you would actually buy.
