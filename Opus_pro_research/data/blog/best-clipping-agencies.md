# The 10 Best Clipping Agencies in 2026

A clipping agency sources and manages a network of short-form editors, called clippers, who cut a brand's long-form content into dozens or hundreds of native TikTok, Reels, and Shorts posts. Most price against verified views rather than a flat retainer, which makes a clipping agency closer to a media buy than a traditional creative shop.

Short-form video didn't just change how people watch. It changed how brands, artists, and creators get discovered. And behind almost every viral moment you've scrolled past on TikTok, Reels, or Shorts that wasn't posted by the original creator, there's a clipping agency running the machine: sourcing clippers, managing distribution, and turning one piece of long-form content into hundreds of native, algorithm-friendly posts.

Clipping agencies have matured fast. What started as scrappy Discord servers paying creators pennies per view has turned into a real industry with CPM benchmarking, verified-view reporting, and campaigns for labels like Universal Music Group and platforms like Netflix. But the agencies aren't interchangeable: they differ a lot in who they serve, how they price, which platforms they prioritize, and how "managed" the experience actually is.

We looked at ten of the clipping agencies creators, brands, and labels are talking about in 2026 to break down what actually makes each one different. Here's what we found.

### How we put this list together

Every agency below is included because it publishes enough about pricing, network size, platform coverage, and past work to be evaluated. We've noted where figures are self-reported by the agency rather than independently verified. The list is not ranked best to worst: the right agency depends almost entirely on your budget tier and content type, so we've ordered it roughly from most managed and highest minimum to most self-serve, and flagged the specific use case each one fits.

## 1. Clipping Culture: best clipping agency for major labels and artists

[Clipping Culture](https://clippingculture.com/), founded by Evan Stanfield and Grayson Peil, runs at the high end of the market. Managed clipping campaigns start at $15,000+, priced against a target volume of *verified* views rather than a flat retainer. Every clip submitted against a campaign is checked against brand guidelines before it counts. That rigor is matched by its client roster: Capitol Records, Universal Music Group, HBO Max, Selena Gomez, Zedd, and bbno$ have all run campaigns through its network of 300,000+ active clippers.

Best for: major labels, established artists, and brand launches that want enterprise-scale distribution and are less price-sensitive than they are results-sensitive. Campaigns reportedly launch within 1–2 days of an intro call.

## 2. The Clip Ship: best clipping agency for music, film, and TV releases

[The Clip Ship](https://www.theclipship.com/) is squarely built for entertainment IP: music releases, film, and television. Its published case studies are some of the most detailed in the space: Logic's Paradise Records rollout generated 125M+ views across 4,000+ clips, The Chainsmokers' Breathe EP hit 108M+ views from 1,100+ clips, and a Netflix campaign for Owning Manhattan drove 25M+ views from 2,400 clips. The agency leans into "growth partner" positioning, transparency on CPM, no arbitrage, and operates a network of 150,000+ creators and clippers.

Best for: labels, film/TV studios, and artist teams that want to see documented, platform-scale case studies before committing budget.

## 3. Clip Farm: best clipping agency for low-commitment, pay-per-view tests

[Clip Farm](https://www.clipfarm.biz/) strips clipping down to its purest performance model: you set your own reward rate per 1,000 views, and you only pay for approved, viewed content: no upfront fees, no retainer. That structure makes it one of the more accessible entry points into paid clipping, and the site pitches itself to everyone from startups to global brands.

What makes Clip Farm particularly interesting is who's behind it: it was founded by Airrack (Eric Decker), the \~14M-subscriber YouTuber known for MrBeast-style challenge videos and viral stunts, launched in partnership with Whop. That's a meaningfully different starting point than most agencies on this list: Clip Farm wasn't built by a marketing or ad-tech team looking to sell brands on a new channel, it was built by a creator who came up on the exact kind of high-volume, algorithm-chasing content this whole industry repackages. That pedigree gives Clip Farm built-in credibility with the clipper side of the marketplace, not just the brand side, which matters in a two-sided platform where you need enough clippers showing up to make any bounty worth posting.

Because Clip Farm runs on Whop, it's worth understanding how that underlying marketplace works before you launch: we break it down in our guide to [Whop Content Rewards](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards).

Best for: teams that want to test clipping as a channel without committing to a large clipping campaign minimum, and that are comfortable managing more of the strategy themselves in exchange for lower cost.

## 4. Zulachat: best clipping marketplace for named-creator reach

[Zulachat](https://zulachat.com/) is less an agency in the traditional sense and more a marketplace connecting brands directly with individual clippers, including some genuinely large ones. The platform highlights creators like David Dobrik and FaZe Rug in its network, with a combined reach north of 158 million followers, and pays out at roughly $100 per 100K views. It's also one of the more transparent platforms on payout mechanics: weekly payouts, PayPal or bank transfer, and top performers reportedly clearing $10K+ a month.

Best for: brands and creators who specifically want TikTok- and Instagram-native distribution (Zula.chat doesn't extend to YouTube Shorts) and like the idea of tapping into named creator accounts rather than an anonymous network.

## 5. Clip Central: best clipping agency for CPM-driven performance marketers

[Clip Central](https://clipcentral.co/) pitches itself with a media-buyer's argument: a direct cost comparison against paid social. Its published numbers put Clip Central at $0.001–$0.004 CPV, versus $0.005–$0.05 for TikTok, Meta, and YouTube ads. That means a 25M-view campaign that might cost $125K–$1.25M in paid media could run $25K–$100K through their network. Pricing itself is CPM-based, with a separate flat rate for their creative studio if you need clips produced, not just distributed. Because no paid spend sits behind the clips, content runs as organic rather than sponsored, and doesn't require #ad disclosure by default (though brands can opt into disclosure language).

Best for: performance marketers who think in CPM/CPV and want a numbers-first pitch for reallocating ad budget toward organic-feeling distribution.

## How to choose a clipping agency

A few patterns emerge once you line these up side by side:

- **If you want documented, big-name case studies**: Clipping Culture and The Clip Ship both lean heavily on named clients and reported view counts.
- **If price and CPM math matter most**: Clip Central makes the clearest cost-per-view argument against paid ads; Clip Farm and Zula.chat both offer pure pay-per-view models with low commitment.
- **If you're a podcaster specifically**: Clipping Agency's dedicated podcast product and CLPR Media's boutique model are worth a look before the generalists.
- **If platform breadth matters**: cliphaus and CLPR Media both push content across four-plus platforms simultaneously.
- **If you want the lowest-commitment way to test the waters**: Bounty Network's no-pitch-deck, hour-to-launch model is built for that.
- **If your source footage is the constraint, not the distribution**: no agency on this list can outrun a slow edit pipeline. Ask any shortlisted agency how source clips get produced and who pays for that work, because on a per-view model the answer directly affects your cost per view.

Two questions are worth asking every agency before you sign: what counts as a verified view, and who produces the clips themselves. Agencies differ enormously on both, and both determine what you're actually buying.

## Clipping agency or clipping marketplace: which do you actually need?

The agencies above sit on a spectrum, and it's worth being clear about which end you're buying from.

**A managed clipping agency** writes the brief, sources footage, vets clippers, reviews every submission, and reports back. You pay a management layer on top of the clipper payouts, and in exchange you're buying judgment: which moments travel, which clippers deliver, which submissions to reject.

**An open clipping marketplace** like Whop Content Rewards, which Clip Farm is built on, removes that layer entirely. You set a rate, fund a budget, and anyone can join. It's the cheapest way to buy views and the most operationally demanding, because the vetting and review work lands on you. Most teams should start self-serve to learn what a viable rate and clip length look like in their niche, then move to a managed agency once the budget is large enough that the management fee costs less than the time it replaces.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a clipping agency?

A clipping agency manages a network of short-form editors who cut a brand's long-form content into native TikTok, Reels, and Shorts posts, then distribute those clips across the clippers' own accounts. Most price against verified views rather than a monthly retainer, which makes the model closer to a performance media buy than a traditional agency engagement.

### How much does a clipping agency cost?

It ranges from effectively nothing upfront to five figures per campaign. Pay-per-view platforms like Clip Farm and Zula.chat charge no upfront fee and pay only for approved views, while fully managed agencies such as Clipping Culture start at $15,000+ per campaign. CPM-based agencies like Clip Central publish cost-per-view figures of $0.001–$0.004, against $0.005–$0.05 for paid social.

### How do clipping agencies work?

The brand supplies long-form source footage and a brief covering tone, hashtags, links, and clip length. The agency distributes that brief to its clipper network, clippers produce and post clips to their own accounts, and the agency reviews submissions against the guidelines before views count toward payout. Payment is typically per 1,000 verified views, capped per clip so budget spreads across more clippers.

### What is the difference between a clipping agency and a clipping marketplace?

An agency adds a management layer: it vets clippers, quality-controls submissions, and handles reporting for a fee. A marketplace like Whop Content Rewards is open to anyone, costs less because there's no management fee, and leaves the sourcing, vetting, and review work with you.

### Are there clipping agencies for podcasts?

Yes. Clipping Agency runs a dedicated podcast product that turns each episode into 10–30 clips, and CLPR Media works with podcasters through its boutique, founder-led model. Both are worth checking before a generalist agency, because podcast clipping depends heavily on finding conversational moments that stand alone without context.

### Do clipping campaigns need #ad disclosure?

It depends on the arrangement, and you should get legal advice on your specific campaign. Agencies including Clip Central note that because no paid media spend sits behind the clips, content runs as organic rather than sponsored and doesn't carry disclosure language by default, though brands can opt into it. Clippers being paid for posts is a material connection, so treat disclosure as a decision to make deliberately rather than a default to inherit.
