# 8 Best Subtitle Generators for Multiple Languages in 2026

For subtitles in more than one language on short-form video, OpusClip is our first pick: its help center lists 25 supported video languages, the captions are animated and styled for vertical posts, and the same project can be clipped, reframed, dubbed and posted. Happy Scribe, Maestra and Sonix are stronger when the priority is the widest language list or subtitle files, VEED and Kapwing suit browser editing with translation, Descript suits transcript editing, and Captions suits phone-first work. We ranked eight tools by how many languages each vendor says it supports, how subtitles get from transcript to finished video, price, free-plan limits and how clearly each vendor documents its limits.

## Key takeaways

- **Best for multilingual captions on short-form video: OpusClip.** Animated captions for 25 languages listed in its help center, plus clipping, reframing and dubbing.
- **Best for the widest spoken-language list: Happy Scribe.** Its pricing page lists AI speech-to-text for 150+ languages and dialects.
- **Best for a free try with 125+ languages: Maestra.** Its subtitle generator page says no account or credit card is needed to start.
- **Best for right-to-left languages and transcript accuracy: Sonix.** Its pages describe Arabic, Hebrew, Persian and Urdu in native scripts. OpusClip's help center says it does not support right-to-left languages.
- **Best for browser editing with subtitle translation: VEED and Kapwing.** Both translate subtitles inside the editor.
- **Check the language count before you commit.** Vendors count transcription, translation and dubbing languages differently, and several publish different numbers on different pages.

## How to choose

Start with what you need the language list to do: transcribe speech, translate text, or dub audio. Those are three different counts.

- **If you mainly need captions in a non-English language on short vertical videos**, look for animated caption styles, reframing and posting in one tool: OpusClip, Captions, VEED.
- **If you mainly need the broadest list of spoken languages**, look for 100+ language transcription: Happy Scribe, Maestra, VEED, Captions.
- **If you mainly need subtitle files (SRT or VTT) for a video player or YouTube**, look for file export in every language: Sonix, Happy Scribe, Maestra, Kapwing.
- **If you mainly need Arabic, Hebrew, Persian or Urdu**, check right-to-left support first: Sonix lists it, and OpusClip does not support it.
- **If you mainly need to translate subtitles into many target languages**, look for a translation count above 50: Kapwing, Happy Scribe, Maestra, Sonix.
- **If you mainly need the voice dubbed, not only subtitled**, look for dubbing languages: OpusClip (Pro), Kapwing, Descript, VEED.

For translation-first needs, see [our subtitle translation tools roundup](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-subtitle-translation-tools); for English-first captioning, see [our AI subtitle generators roundup](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-ai-subtitle-generators); for voice, see [our AI dubbing tools roundup](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-ai-dubbing-tools).

## 1. OpusClip: Best for multilingual captions on short-form video

*The video languages list in OpusClip's help center. Source: [OpusClip Help Center](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/video-languages-supported)*

OpusClip's [help center language list](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/video-languages-supported) says it supports over 25 languages, including English, German, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Italian, Dutch, Russian, Polish, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese,, with more listed as coming. The help center marks many of those languages as beta, which it says can mean inaccurate emojis or highlighted keywords. You submit a long video or a link, and [OpusClip's captions feature](https://www.opus.pro/captions) adds animated captions in the spoken language, with a text and timeline editor to correct words afterward.

Because the captions sit inside a clipping and reformatting workflow, one long recording becomes several vertical clips with captions already on them, reframed to 9:16, 1:1, 4:5 or 16:9. You can also upload your own SRT file when you already have a translation, and Pro users can download subtitles as an SRT file, per the help center. For spoken translation, [Video Dubbing](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/video-dubbing) translates and dubs a video into 25 languages using a clone of your own voice, on Pro and Business.

**Pricing:** Per opus.pro/pricing, Free is $0 with 60 credits a month, up to 1080p clips, a watermark and no editing. Starter is $15 a month (monthly billing only) with 150 credits. Pro is $29 a month, or $14.50 a month billed annually, with 300 credits, a social scheduler and video dubbing. Business is custom. The help center lists dubbing at 10 credits a minute on Pro and Business.

**Considerations:** The help center says OpusClip does not support right-to-left languages such as Hebrew, Kurdish, Arabic or Persian. Translating captions before clipping currently supports English as the target language only, so translation into other languages goes through dubbing or your own SRT. The 25-language list is shorter than the 100+ lists below, and OpusClip is a clip-and-publish tool, not a replacement for a full non-linear editor.

- **Choose it if** your videos are short-form, the spoken language is on the list, and you want animated captions, reframing and posting from one project.
- **Skip it if** you need Arabic or Hebrew, a language outside the list, or subtitles translated into many target languages.

## Red flags to watch for

### Language counts that mean different things

One vendor's "125+ languages" may be transcription, another's may be translation or dubbing, and some vendors give different numbers on different pages. **Trial test:** upload a short clip in your actual spoken language and export subtitles in your actual target language before comparing plans.

### Beta or limited language support

OpusClip's help center marks many of its listed languages as beta, and Descript marks caption translation as Limited on its Free plan. **Trial test:** check the caption text for names and technical terms in your language, not just a sample sentence.

### Right-to-left languages

OpusClip's help center says it does not support Hebrew, Kurdish, Arabic or Persian, while Sonix lists native right-to-left output. **Trial test:** confirm the text renders in the right direction on the exported video, not only in the editor.

### Watermarks and minute limits on free exports

OpusClip Free and Kapwing Free add watermarks, Kapwing Free caps exports at 4 minutes, and Happy Scribe Free watermarks MP4 export. **Trial test:** export one finished video before deciding a free plan is enough.

### Metered hours and credits

Sonix and Happy Scribe meter transcription and translation in hours or minutes, VEED Creator caps subtitles at 120 hours a year, and Kapwing meters subtitling and dubbing in credits. **Trial test:** estimate a normal month of footage against the allowance, including each translated language.

### Prices you cannot confirm

Captions' page shows iOS plans only, Maestra splits pricing across product tabs, and several pages have monthly and annual toggles. **Trial test:** check the price at checkout before comparing plans.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best software for video captions in different languages?

For short-form video, OpusClip is our first pick: its help center lists 25 supported video languages, with animated captions, reframing, dubbing and posting in the same project. For the longest language lists, Happy Scribe (150+ per its pricing page) and Maestra (125+) go further. See [OpusClip's captions feature](https://www.opus.pro/captions).

### Where can I find the best tools to add subtitles to a video?

Browser editors such as VEED and Kapwing let you add and translate subtitles on an uploaded video, subtitling services such as Happy Scribe, Maestra and Sonix produce subtitle files, and OpusClip adds animated captions while clipping long videos. You can try a free option first through [OpusClip's free auto caption generator](https://www.opus.pro/tools/auto-caption-generator).

### Which subtitle generator supports the most languages?

On vendor pages, Happy Scribe lists AI speech-to-text for 150+ languages and dialects, Maestra and VEED list 125+, and Captions lists 100+ for captions. These are vendor claims and count different things, so test your own language.

### Can OpusClip add captions in languages other than English?

Yes. Its help center lists over 25 supported video languages, and its captions are generated in the spoken language. The help center says captions can be translated before clipping to English only, and that right-to-left languages are not supported. See the [full language list](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/video-languages-supported).

### Can I dub a video into another language instead of subtitling it?

OpusClip's Video Dubbing translates and dubs a video into 25 languages using a clone of your voice, and it is available on Pro and Business. Kapwing, Descript and VEED also list dubbing. See [our AI dubbing tools roundup](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-ai-dubbing-tools).

### What is the best free subtitle generator for multiple languages?

Maestra says its subtitle generator is free to try without an account, Happy Scribe and Sonix offer free trials, and OpusClip Free includes 60 credits a month with a watermark. Check watermarks and length limits before relying on any free plan.

### Which subtitle generator works for Arabic or Hebrew?

Sonix's pages describe native right-to-left output for Arabic, Hebrew, Persian and Urdu. OpusClip's help center says it does not support right-to-left languages, so pick another tool for those languages.

### Are subtitles the same as captions?

Subtitles usually translate or transcribe the dialogue for viewers who can hear the audio, while captions also cover sound cues, and many tools use the words interchangeably. See [caption vs subtitle](https://www.opus.pro/blog/caption-vs-subtitle).
