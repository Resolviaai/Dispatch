# 8 Best Text-Based Video Editors in 2026 (Compared & Ranked)

The best text-based video editor depends on what is being edited. Descript is built around the idea from the ground up, so it suits long recordings edited from scratch. Adobe Premiere suits editors who want a transcript inside a full timeline editor, and Riverside suits podcasters who record and edit in one place. OpusClip is ranked first for a narrower job: cutting the short clips it generates from a long video by deleting words in the transcript, then posting them. We ranked eight tools by the job each does best, then by price, free-tier limits and how clearly each vendor documents its text editing.

## Key takeaways

- **Best for editing the short clips cut from a long video, then posting them: OpusClip.** Select words in the transcript, click Delete, and the footage goes with them.
- **Best for editing a whole recording by editing the text: Descript.** Its own site describes deleting a word as deleting that moment of video.
- **Best for text-based editing inside a full timeline editor: Adobe Premiere.** It transcribes on import and builds a rough cut from the text.
- **Best for podcasts and interviews recorded online: Riverside.** Recording, transcript editing and clips live in one product.
- **Best for YouTube talking-head footage on a desktop: Gling.** It removes bad takes, silences and filler words, then lets the edit be refined by text or timeline.
- **Best free starting points:** Descript Free, Kapwing Free (watermark), VEED Free (watermark), Gling Free (watermark) and Vizard Free. Check watermarks, export resolution and monthly limits first.

## How to choose

Start with the job done most often, then match it to the tools built for it.

- **If the job is editing a long recording from scratch by deleting and moving text, look for a text-first editor:** Descript.
- **If the job is turning a long video into short posts and fixing the clips by text, look for clipping plus a transcript editor plus publishing:** OpusClip, Vizard.
- **If the job is professional timeline work that starts from a transcript, look for text-based editing inside a full editor:** Adobe Premiere.
- **If the job is recording remote guests and editing the episode, look for recording and text editing together:** Riverside.
- **If the job is editing in a browser with a team, look for shared workspaces:** Kapwing, VEED.
- **If the job is cleaning up solo talking-head takes on a desktop, look for automatic take, silence and filler removal:** Gling.
- **If the budget is zero, look for a free plan with a usable export:** Descript Free, Vizard Free, and the watermarked free plans from Kapwing, VEED, Gling and OpusClip.

## 1. OpusClip: Best for editing the clips cut from a long video by text, then posting them

*Adding a long video in OpusClip. Source: [OpusClip Help Center](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/how-to-use-clipanything)*

OpusClip takes a long video (a podcast, webinar, interview or livestream) and returns short clips, each with a Virality Score from 0 to 99. Its in-app editor handles text-based and timeline-based editing, according to the OpusClip help center. To cut a sentence, select it in the transcript and click Delete, or drag the segment's ends in the timeline. Words and whole sentences can also be corrected, and a caption-only delete leaves the video untouched. A section from elsewhere in the source video can be added from the full transcript.

Speech Cleanup highlights filler words in the transcript and cuts the ones that are checked, and pauses can be removed with an adjustable threshold. Finished clips can be auto-posted to YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Instagram Reels from the Starter plan, and the Pro plan adds a social scheduler and XML export to Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve.

**Pricing:** Per [opus.pro/pricing](https://www.opus.pro/pricing), Free is $0 with 60 credits a month, a watermark and no editing, so the text and timeline editor is a paid-plan feature. Starter is $15 a month (monthly billing only) with 150 credits and one brand template. Pro is $29 a month, or $14.50 a month billed annually, with 300 credits that roll over for 2 months. Business is custom.

**Considerations:** The editor works on the clips OpusClip generates from a longer video, so it is not the tool for editing a two-hour recording as one continuous project. OpusClip is a clip-and-publish tool, not a replacement for a full non-linear editor. Multi-track timeline work, heavy color grading and effects belong in a tool like Premiere or DaVinci Resolve, and Pro can export XML to either.

- **Choose it if** the job is turning long recordings into short posts and fixing each clip by deleting words, then publishing.
- **Skip it if** the job is editing one long recording end to end, or the plan is Free, which has no editing.

## 2. Descript: Best for editing a whole recording by editing the transcript

*Text-based editing in Descript. Source: Descript website*

Descript is the tool most completely built around editing by text. Its own FAQ says that it transcribes a recording and that deleting a word deletes that moment of video, while moving a sentence moves the footage with it. Its pricing page lists Underlord (its AI video co-editor), Studio Sound, Remove Filler Words and Create Clips among the AI tools. Its free plan is described as including text-based editing.

For a single long project such as a podcast episode, tutorial or interview, Descript is the stronger pick on this list, and it is the one we would point most people to if the whole job is editing by text. Its site says a timeline is available for fine control, but a whole video can be made without touching it.

**Pricing:** Descript's pricing page lists Free at $0 with 60 media minutes a month and 720p export. Hobbyist is $16 per person a month on annual billing ($24 monthly) with 10 media hours, 400 AI credits and 1080p watermark-free export. Creator is $24 a month on annual billing ($35 monthly) with 30 media hours, 800 AI credits and 4K export. Business is $50 a month on annual billing ($65 monthly). Enterprise is custom.

**Considerations:** Usage is metered in media hours and AI credits, so heavy months can run past a plan. Descript's focus is editing a project, not producing a batch of short clips and scheduling them to social accounts.

- **Choose it if** the job is editing long spoken recordings by deleting and moving text.
- **Skip it if** the job is repurposing one recording into many short posts with built-in publishing.

## 3. Adobe Premiere: Best for text-based editing inside a professional timeline editor

*Adobe Premiere. Source: Adobe website*

Adobe describes Text-Based Editing in Premiere as a way to refine, reorder and trim clips like editing a text document. Its Premiere page says AI transcribes video on import, and a rough cut can be made by copying and pasting text. Adobe's help center describes enabling automatic transcription in the import settings, choosing language and speaker labeling options, and then opening the Text-Based Editing workspace to view transcripts. Its help pages also list detecting and deleting pauses in transcripts and removing every instance of one speaker.

The advantage over a text-first tool is that the same project sits in a full professional timeline with color, audio and effects tools.

**Pricing:** Adobe's Premiere page lists US$22.99 a month for Premiere (annual plan, billed monthly) and offers a free trial. Creative Cloud Pro, which bundles Premiere with other apps, is listed separately on the same page.

**Considerations:** Premiere is a full non-linear editor with a steeper learning curve than a text-first tool, and the text workflow is one feature inside it. It does not generate or schedule short social clips the way a clipper does.

- **Choose it if** the edit needs a transcript-driven rough cut and a full timeline afterward.
- **Skip it if** the goal is a quick text edit with no timeline software to learn.

Unlimited text-based editing is listed under the Pro plan, not Free, so check what a free trial covers before planning around it. ## Red flags to watch for

### Transcript edits that do not move the video

Some tools let the text be corrected for captions without changing the footage. OpusClip, for example, offers both a caption-only delete and a delete that removes the video too. **Trial test:** delete one sentence and play it back to confirm the picture and sound are cut, not only the caption.

### Text editing locked behind a paid plan

OpusClip's Free plan has no editing, and Riverside lists unlimited text-based editing under Pro. **Trial test:** open the editor on the plan being considered and make one text cut before paying.

### Watermarks and low-resolution free exports

Riverside, Kapwing, VEED, Gling and OpusClip list watermarks on their free plans, and several cap free exports at 720p. **Trial test:** export a finished video before deciding the free plan is enough.

### Short export caps and metered hours

Kapwing Free caps exports at 4 minutes, Gling's hours do not carry over, and VEED credits do not carry over. **Trial test:** run a normal-length recording, not a short demo clip, against the monthly allowance.

### No timeline for the last 10 percent

Text edits are fast for cuts but not for layout, audio mixing or color. **Trial test:** check that the tool has a timeline or an export path to a full editor for the final polish. OpusClip Pro and Gling both list XML export to professional editors.

### Quality of the transcript itself

A text edit is only as good as the transcription underneath it. **Trial test:** upload a recording with accents, jargon or crosstalk and check how many words need correcting before editing begins.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best text-based video editor?

For editing an entire recording by editing the transcript, Descript is the pick, since it is built around that idea. For editing the short clips cut from a long video and then posting them, OpusClip fits best. For text-based editing inside a full timeline editor, Adobe Premiere is the pick, and for podcasts recorded online, Riverside.

### Can I edit video by editing text?

Yes. In a text-based editor the video is transcribed, and deleting words in the transcript cuts that footage from the video. Descript, Adobe Premiere, Riverside, Kapwing, VEED, Gling, Vizard and OpusClip all describe a version of this on their own pages. See [OpusClip's help article on editing and trimming by text](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/change-captions).

### Is there a free text-based video editor?

Several have free plans. Descript's pricing page describes its free plan as including text-based editing, with 60 media minutes a month and 720p export. Kapwing, VEED and Gling list free plans with watermarks, and Vizard lists a free plan with 60 credits a month and 720p export. OpusClip's Free plan has no editing, so text editing there starts on a paid plan from $15 a month. See [OpusClip pricing](https://www.opus.pro/pricing).

### Is text-based editing better than timeline editing?

It is faster for cutting spoken content because the words are visible. Timelines are better for layout, multi-track audio, color and effects. Several tools here offer both: OpusClip, Vizard, Gling and Adobe Premiere pair text editing with a timeline.

### Can text-based editing remove filler words?

Yes, in tools that document it. Descript lists Remove Filler Words, Riverside lists removing filler words and silences, OpusClip's Speech Cleanup highlights fillers and pauses in the transcript, and Gling lists filler-word removal. For more, see [our AI silence removers roundup](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-ai-silence-removers).

### Which text-based editor is best for podcasts?

Riverside records remote guests on separate tracks and edits them by transcript. Descript suits editing a finished podcast recording by text. For turning an episode into short social clips, OpusClip and Vizard are built for that job.

### Does OpusClip have a text editor?

Yes, on paid plans. Per the OpusClip help center and pricing page, the in-app editor supports text-based and timeline-based editing of the clips OpusClip generates, but the Free plan has no editing. OpusClip is not a replacement for a full non-linear editor.
