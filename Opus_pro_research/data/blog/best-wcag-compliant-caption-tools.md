## Why WCAG Compliance Matters for Your Video Captions

WCAG compliance isn't just legal jargon; it's a framework that ensures your content reaches everyone. The guidelines are organized into three levels: A (minimum), AA (mid-range), and AAA (highest). Most organizations aim for AA compliance, which covers the essential accessibility features that make a real difference. When your captions meet these standards, you're not just avoiding potential lawsuits; you're opening your content to millions of people who rely on captions daily.

I learned this the hard way when a major brand partnership almost fell through because my captions didn't meet their accessibility requirements. They needed WCAG 2.1 AA compliance, which meant my captions had to include accurate timing (synchronized within one second), proper punctuation, speaker identification, and descriptions of relevant sounds. Auto-generated captions from social platforms rarely cut it because they miss these nuances. A compliant caption file shows who's speaking when multiple people talk, describes important audio cues like laughter or music, and maintains readability with proper line breaks and character limits.

The business case is equally compelling. Search engines index your captions, boosting your SEO. Social media algorithms favor accessible content. Your completion rates improve because viewers can follow along in noisy environments or silent spaces. Plus, you're future-proofing your content as accessibility regulations tighten globally. The European Accessibility Act, ADA requirements in the US, and similar laws worldwide are making WCAG compliance mandatory for more organizations every year.

### Key WCAG Requirements for Video Captions

Understanding what makes captions compliant helps you evaluate tools effectively. WCAG 2.1 Level AA requires captions to be synchronized with audio, accurate in content, and complete (covering all spoken dialogue and relevant sounds). Captions must appear on screen long enough to be read comfortably, typically following the "160 words per minute" rule for reading speed. They should identify speakers when it's not obvious from context, and they must describe important non-speech audio like sound effects or music that's essential to understanding the content.

Formatting matters too. Compliant captions use proper punctuation and capitalization, break lines at logical points (not mid-phrase), and maintain a maximum of 32 characters per line with no more than three lines on screen simultaneously. The text must have sufficient contrast against the background, and viewers should be able to customize caption appearance when possible. These technical specifications ensure that captions are readable and useful, not just present.

### Best Practices for YouTube Caption Editing

To make YouTube's auto-captions WCAG-compliant, focus on these editing priorities. First, review the entire transcript for accuracy, correcting misheard words and adding proper punctuation. Second, add speaker labels when multiple people appear, using brackets like \"\[Sarah\]\" before dialogue. Third, include sound descriptions for important audio cues, such as \"\[applause\]\" or \"\[music playing\]\". Finally, check timing by watching the video with captions enabled, adjusting any segments where captions appear too early, too late, or remain on screen too briefly for comfortable reading. This systematic approach transforms auto-captions into compliant, professional results.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between subtitles and WCAG-compliant captions?

Subtitles typically only include spoken dialogue and assume viewers can hear audio, while WCAG-compliant captions include speaker identification, sound descriptions, and music cues for viewers who cannot hear. Captions are designed for accessibility, covering all audio information necessary to understand the content. Subtitles often appear in different languages for translation purposes, whereas captions match the video's original language and provide complete audio context.

### Do auto-generated captions from social platforms meet WCAG standards?

Auto-generated captions from platforms like Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok rarely meet WCAG compliance standards without editing. They typically lack proper punctuation, miss speaker identification, contain accuracy errors, and don't include sound descriptions. These auto-captions provide a starting point but require significant refinement to achieve true accessibility compliance. For content where accessibility is important, always review and edit auto-generated captions or use dedicated captioning tools designed for WCAG compliance.

### How much does professional WCAG-compliant captioning cost?

Professional human captioning typically costs between one and three dollars per video minute, depending on turnaround time and complexity. Automated captioning services range from five to twenty-five cents per minute but require editing for full compliance. Subscription-based tools like OpusClip, Descript, or Sonix offer unlimited captioning within monthly plans ranging from fifteen to fifty dollars, making them cost-effective for high-volume creators. The true cost includes both the tool expense and the time you invest in reviewing and editing for accuracy.

### Can I use the same caption file across different video platforms?

Most platforms support standard caption formats like SRT or VTT, allowing you to use the same caption file across YouTube, Vimeo, Facebook, and your website. However, you may need to adjust styling or formatting for optimal display on each platform. Some platforms have character limits or formatting restrictions that require modifications. Tools like OpusClip can generate platform-specific versions automatically, while others require manual export in different formats. Always test captions on each platform to ensure they display correctly and remain readable.

### How long does it take to create WCAG-compliant captions?

Automated transcription takes minutes, but editing for WCAG compliance typically requires one to three times the video length depending on audio quality and complexity. A ten-minute video might need thirty minutes of editing to achieve full compliance. Human captioning services deliver in twelve to twenty-four hours. Real-time tools like Otter.ai provide immediate captions during live events but still need post-event editing. Your efficiency improves with practice; after captioning dozens of videos, you'll develop workflows that reduce editing time significantly.

### Are there legal requirements for video captions?

Legal requirements vary by country and content type. In the United States, the ADA requires captions for public-facing content from many organizations, and Section 508 mandates accessibility for federal agencies. The European Accessibility Act will require captions for many digital services starting in 2025. Educational institutions receiving federal funding must provide captions under ADA and Section 504. Even without legal obligations, providing WCAG-compliant captions protects against discrimination claims and demonstrates commitment to inclusive content. Consult legal counsel for specific requirements affecting your organization.

### What accuracy percentage is required for WCAG compliance?

WCAG doesn't specify an exact accuracy percentage, but industry standards typically aim for 99% accuracy for compliance. This means no more than one error per 100 words. The accuracy requirement focuses on meaning; captions must convey the same information as the audio without misleading viewers. Minor errors in filler words may be acceptable, but mistakes in key terms, names, or numbers compromise compliance. For critical content like legal, medical, or educational materials, aim for the highest accuracy possible through human captioning or thorough editing of automated transcriptions.
