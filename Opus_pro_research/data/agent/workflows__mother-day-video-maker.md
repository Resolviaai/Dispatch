### What are best practices for prompts in a mother day video maker?

Start with clarity about your goal. Specificity helps the AI choose the right pacing, visuals, and music. If you're uploading photos, mention them in the prompt so the system knows to prioritize those images over stock footage. For example, 'Use the three family photos I uploaded as the main visuals, with soft transitions and a heartfelt voiceover.' If you want product integration, say 'Feature our jewelry product shots with elegant motion graphics and a call to action at the end.' Include emotional keywords that guide tone. Words like 'nostalgic,' 'joyful,' 'grateful,' or 'celebratory' signal the AI to adjust music, animation speed, and visual effects. For scripts, structure them with clear beats. Write something like 'Opening: Thank you, Mom, for always being there. Middle: Share story about childhood memory. Closing: I love you and can't wait to celebrate together.' This gives the AI a narrative framework to build scenes around. If you're working from a blog post or article, paste the URL and add a note like 'Turn this Mother's Day gift guide into a 90-second video with upbeat music and product highlights.' The AI will extract key points and visualize them. Avoid vague prompts like 'Make a Mother's Day video.' Instead, describe the outcome you want. Length matters too. Specify '30 seconds for Instagram Reels' or '2 minutes for YouTube' so the AI paces content appropriately. Test different prompt styles. A bullet-point outline works as well as a full script. The mother day video maker is flexible, but the more context you provide about audience, platform, and purpose, the better the output aligns with your vision.
