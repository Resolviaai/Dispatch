### What are best practices for prompts when using a breaking news video maker?

Effective prompts for a breaking news video maker start with clarity about the core story. Lead with the headline or main fact, then provide 2-3 supporting details. For example: 'Breaking: Tech company announces major acquisition. Include stock price impact, CEO quote, and industry reaction.' The more specific your prompt, the better the visual assembly. Mention visual elements you want featured, like 'show company logos,' 'include chart of stock movement,' or 'use cityscape B-roll.' If you have brand guidelines, reference them: 'use our red and white color scheme' or 'include our logo in lower third.' For voice and tone, specify the delivery style: 'urgent news anchor tone' or 'calm, authoritative explanation.' The breaking news video maker responds to emotional cues, so words like 'shocking,' 'historic,' or 'unprecedented' influence pacing and visual drama. If you're working from a script, paste the full narration text and add a line about desired visuals: 'Script below. Use motion graphics for statistics, stock footage for locations mentioned, and urgent alert styling.' For article URLs, the system extracts content automatically, but you can guide it: 'Focus on the economic impact section' or 'Emphasize the human interest angle.' Always specify your target platform and length: '30 seconds for Instagram Reels' or '90 seconds for YouTube.' This helps the breaking news video maker optimize aspect ratio, pacing, and visual density for where your audience will watch.

### What are the limitations of a breaking news video maker for complex or sensitive stories?

A breaking news video maker like Agent Opus handles most news stories effectively, but understanding its boundaries helps you get the best results. For highly complex stories with multiple threads, the system works best when you break the narrative into clear segments in your prompt. Instead of 'Explain the entire political crisis,' try 'Focus on the three main events: the initial announcement, public reaction, and government response.' This gives the AI clear structure to follow. For sensitive topics, the breaking news video maker generates appropriate visuals but relies on your editorial judgment for tone and framing. You control the script and narration, so you maintain full editorial responsibility. The system won't inject opinions or bias, it visualizes the story you provide. If you're covering tragedy or conflict, specify the tone: 'somber, respectful tone with minimal motion graphics' versus 'urgent breaking alert style.' The AI responds to these cues. One limitation is real-time footage. The breaking news video maker sources stock and web images, but it cannot access live news feeds or proprietary footage. If your story requires specific on-the-ground video, you'll need to source that separately. However, for most breaking news, the combination of motion graphics, stock footage, and dynamic text overlays creates professional results without custom footage. Another consideration is fact-checking. The system generates videos based on your input, so accuracy depends on your source material. Always verify facts before generating, especially for breaking stories where details evolve quickly. The breaking news video maker is a production tool, not a reporting tool. Finally, extremely short turnaround stories under 10 seconds can feel rushed. The system needs enough time to establish context, deliver the news, and provide visual interest. For ultra-short alerts, focus on a single bold headline with minimal supporting detail.
