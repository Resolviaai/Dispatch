### What are best practices for prompts when using the Stephen Curry AI video generator?

Effective prompts for the Stephen Curry AI video generator balance specificity with creative freedom. Start with the core concept: 'Create a 60-second video showing Stephen Curry's shooting technique for basketball training.' Then add context layers. Specify the audience—'for youth players learning fundamentals' or 'for advanced athletes refining their form.' Include mood and pacing cues: 'fast-paced and energetic' for highlight content, or 'slow and instructional' for technique breakdowns. Mention visual elements you want: 'include slow-motion shooting form, court diagrams showing spacing, and motivational text overlays.' If you have brand requirements, state them upfront: 'feature our basketball training app logo in the lower third and show our product in the final call-to-action.' The AI responds well to structured prompts that separate sections—intro hook, main content, and closing CTA. For example: 'Open with a quick montage of three-pointers, transition to a breakdown of shooting mechanics with voiceover, close with a call to download our training guide.' You can also reference specific Curry moments or playing styles: 'capture the energy of his 2016 season' or 'emphasize his off-ball movement and quick release.' Avoid overly vague prompts like 'make a basketball video'—the more detail you provide about intent, audience, and desired outcome, the better the Stephen Curry AI video generator performs. If you're working from a script, include speaker notes or visual cues in brackets. The system interprets these as scene direction. Test different prompt styles to find what works for your content type, and remember that you can iterate—generate a first version, then refine your prompt based on what you see.
