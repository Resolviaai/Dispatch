## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does images to video generation handle different photo types and quality levels?

Agent Opus processes any image format and resolution, from high-resolution product photography to smartphone snapshots, social media graphics, and logo files. The system analyzes each image's composition, subject matter, and visual weight to determine optimal placement within your video sequence. Low-resolution images receive intelligent upscaling and enhancement to maintain professional output quality, while high-resolution photos are optimized for web delivery without losing detail. The AI identifies focal points in each image, whether that's a product, person, text overlay, or design element, and applies motion graphics that draw viewer attention to those key areas. Mixed-quality image sets are automatically balanced so transitions feel cohesive rather than jarring. If you're working with product catalogs, the system recognizes similar items and creates visual groupings that make sense narratively. For brand assets like logos and graphics, Agent Opus maintains aspect ratios and applies subtle animation that adds polish without overwhelming your design. The motion graphics engine adapts its approach based on image content: lifestyle photos get cinematic pans and zooms, infographics receive highlight animations that guide the eye through data points, and text-heavy images hold longer on screen with gentle movement that maintains readability. You can upload images in any order, and the AI will suggest optimal sequencing based on visual flow, color harmony, and narrative logic, though you retain control to override these suggestions through your prompt or script instructions.

### What are best practices for writing prompts when converting images to video?

Effective images to video prompts balance creative direction with technical clarity. Start by describing your video's purpose and target audience, such as 'Create a 60-second product launch video for Instagram Reels targeting millennial home decor enthusiasts' or 'Generate a professional portfolio showcase for LinkedIn highlighting architectural photography.' This context helps Agent Opus select appropriate pacing, tone, and motion styles. Next, specify how you want your images sequenced. You can request chronological order ('Show the product evolution from sketch to final design'), thematic grouping ('Group all outdoor shots together, then transition to indoor scenes'), or emotional progression ('Start with problem images, build to solution reveal'). Include voiceover guidance by describing the narrative style: 'Energetic voiceover explaining each feature' or 'Calm, authoritative narration walking through the process.' If you want specific images emphasized, reference them by filename or description: 'Spend extra time on hero-product-shot.jpg with dramatic zoom' or 'Use the logo as a recurring visual anchor between sections.' Specify desired video length, which influences how long each image appears and how many motion effects are applied. Shorter videos (15-30 seconds) work best with 5-8 key images and punchy transitions, while longer formats (60-90 seconds) can accommodate 12-20 images with more elaborate motion graphics. Mention any brand guidelines: 'Maintain minimal, clean aesthetic consistent with our Scandinavian design brand' or 'Use bold, high-energy transitions matching our sports apparel identity.' For voice selection, describe the tone you need: 'Professional female voice, mid-30s, confident but approachable' or 'Use my cloned voice for authentic founder story.' The more specific your prompt, the closer the first generation will match your vision, though Agent Opus handles vague prompts intelligently by making smart defaults based on image content and common use cases.

### How does the system handle images with text, infographics, or data visualizations?

Agent Opus applies specialized processing when it detects text-heavy images, infographics, charts, or data visualizations in your upload set. The AI performs optical character recognition to understand text content and hierarchy, ensuring that motion graphics enhance rather than obscure readability. For infographics, the system identifies individual data points, chart sections, and callout boxes, then applies sequential highlight animations that guide viewers through the information in logical order. A bar chart might animate with each bar growing in sequence while the voiceover explains what each represents. Pie charts can rotate into view with segments highlighting as they're discussed. Text-based slides receive subtle motion that maintains focus: gentle zooms that draw attention to key phrases, or light parallax effects that add depth without causing motion sickness. The pacing automatically adjusts for text-heavy images, holding them on screen longer than purely visual photos to give viewers adequate reading time. Agent Opus calculates reading time based on word count and adjusts both the visual duration and voiceover pacing to match. If your script mentions specific data points or text elements, the system synchronizes visual highlights with those verbal references, creating a cohesive educational experience. For comparison images (before/after, old vs new, competitor analysis), the AI can apply split-screen effects, side-by-side reveals, or sequential presentation based on your prompt instructions. Screenshots and UI mockups receive special treatment: the system can simulate scrolling through a webpage, clicking through an app interface, or zooming into specific features, making software demos and product tutorials feel interactive. When mixing text-heavy images with photographs, Agent Opus creates natural transitions that shift viewer attention from reading mode to visual absorption, using motion cues and pacing changes to signal these shifts. The voiceover integration is particularly sophisticated with data visualizations, as the AI ensures spoken numbers and statistics align precisely with their visual appearance, reinforcing comprehension through multi-sensory presentation.
