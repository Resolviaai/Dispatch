### How does a spreadsheet to video generator handle different data types and formats?

A spreadsheet to video generator like Agent Opus works by interpreting the narrative and context you provide about your data, not by directly parsing spreadsheet files. You describe the key metrics, trends, comparisons, or insights you want to visualize. For example, you might write: 'Our Q4 revenue grew 34% year-over-year, driven by a 52% increase in enterprise accounts and 28% growth in average deal size.' Agent Opus then generates animated charts, callouts, and motion graphics that bring those numbers to life. You can also paste formatted data summaries, bullet points of findings, or even a script that references specific figures. The system excels at turning qualitative data stories into visual sequences. If you have charts already created in Excel or Google Sheets, you can describe them or reference screenshot URLs, and Agent Opus will source similar visuals or generate animated equivalents. The flexibility means you're not locked into a specific spreadsheet format. Whether you're working with sales dashboards, survey results, financial reports, or product analytics, the spreadsheet to video generator adapts to your storytelling needs. Best practice: frame your data as a narrative first. Instead of uploading raw CSV files, write a brief that explains what the numbers mean, why they matter, and what visual emphasis you want. Agent Opus handles the rest, building scenes that highlight trends, compare metrics, and guide viewers through your data story with professional pacing and motion design.

### What are best practices for prompts when using a spreadsheet to video generator?

Effective prompts for a spreadsheet to video generator focus on context, emphasis, and narrative flow. Start by stating the core insight or headline: 'Customer retention improved 18% after launching our new onboarding flow.' Then provide supporting data points: 'Day-7 activation rose from 42% to 61%, and 30-day churn dropped from 22% to 15%.' This gives Agent Opus the structure to build a logical visual sequence. Next, specify the tone and audience. Are you presenting to investors, celebrating wins with your team, or educating customers? A prompt like 'Create an upbeat 45-second video for LinkedIn showing how our Q3 product improvements drove measurable retention gains' sets clear creative direction. Include any visual preferences: 'Use animated bar charts to compare before-and-after metrics, highlight the 18% improvement with a bold callout, and show our product logo in the opening frame.' If you want a specific voiceover style, mention it: 'Use a confident, professional AI voice' or 'Clone my voice for a personal founder update.' For complex data stories, break your prompt into scenes: 'Scene 1: Introduce the challenge (high churn). Scene 2: Show the solution (new onboarding). Scene 3: Reveal the results (retention metrics). Scene 4: Call to action (learn more).' Agent Opus will pace and transition these automatically. Avoid vague prompts like 'Make a video about my data.' Instead, be specific about what you want viewers to learn, feel, or do. The more narrative detail you provide, the more polished and purposeful your spreadsheet to video output becomes.

### What are the limitations or edge cases of a spreadsheet to video generator?

A spreadsheet to video generator like Agent Opus is optimized for narrative-driven data storytelling, not raw spreadsheet file parsing. It does not directly import Excel or Google Sheets files to auto-generate charts. Instead, you describe or paste the data insights you want visualized, and Agent Opus builds the video from that context. This means the tool works best when you've already identified the key metrics, trends, or comparisons worth highlighting. If you need to explore raw data interactively or generate dozens of chart variations, you'll want to do that analysis in your spreadsheet software first, then bring the finalized insights to Agent Opus. Another edge case: highly technical or niche data visualizations like 3D scatter plots, heatmaps, or complex statistical models may require simplified descriptions. Agent Opus excels at common chart types (bar, line, pie, comparison tables) and motion graphics that emphasize key numbers. If your data story relies on specialized academic or scientific visualizations, consider describing the takeaway in plain language rather than expecting pixel-perfect chart replication. The spreadsheet to video generator also assumes you want a finished, publish-ready video. There's no timeline editor or manual scene trimming. If you need granular control over every frame, transition, or visual element, Agent Opus may feel too automated. However, for most creators and marketers, this is a feature, not a limitation. The goal is speed and simplicity: one prompt, one polished video. Finally, while Agent Opus supports voice cloning and avatars, it requires clear audio input for cloning and may not perfectly replicate highly unique vocal characteristics. Test your voice clone on a short data video first to ensure it meets your quality standards before scaling to larger projects.
