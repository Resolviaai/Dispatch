### What are best practices for prompts in an email video maker?

To get the best results from Agent Opus as an email video maker, structure your prompts with clarity and context. Start by defining the goal: are you announcing a product, explaining a feature, sharing a testimonial, or nurturing a lead? For example, instead of 'Make a video about our new app,' write 'Create a 30-second email video announcing our project management app launch, highlighting real-time collaboration, mobile access, and a 14-day free trial.' This gives Agent Opus the narrative arc, key messages, and call-to-action it needs to assemble scenes effectively. If you have a script, paste it directly. The email video maker will parse speaker notes, scene descriptions, and timing cues. If you are working from an outline, use bullet points to indicate scene breaks: 'Scene 1: Problem statement. Scene 2: Solution demo. Scene 3: CTA.' Agent Opus interprets these as visual segments and paces the video accordingly. When you provide a blog post URL, the system extracts headings, subheadings, and key paragraphs, then converts them into a video narrative. For brand consistency, mention visual preferences in your prompt: 'Use our logo in the intro, show product screenshots in the middle, and end with a team photo.' Upload those assets before generating, and Agent Opus will integrate them seamlessly. For voiceover, specify tone and style: 'Use a professional female AI voice' or 'Clone my voice for a personal touch.' If you want an avatar, note whether it should be AI-generated or use your own footage. The more specific you are about pacing, tone, and visual elements, the closer the first draft will be to your vision. However, even minimal prompts work well because the email video maker is designed to infer structure and apply best practices automatically. Test different prompt styles to see what works for your workflow, and remember that Agent Opus optimizes for email-friendly video: short, engaging, and mobile-responsive.

### What are the limitations or edge cases of an email video maker like Agent Opus?

Agent Opus is a powerful email video maker, but understanding its scope helps you set realistic expectations and plan workflows effectively. First, Agent Opus is a text-to-video generation system, not a manual editing tool. You provide input as a prompt, script, outline, or blog URL, and the system generates a finished video. There is no timeline interface, no trimming controls, and no frame-by-frame editing. If you need to adjust a specific scene, you refine your input prompt and regenerate rather than tweaking the output directly. This is by design: the email video maker prioritizes speed and automation over granular control. For most email campaigns, this trade-off is ideal because you get publish-ready videos in minutes. However, if your use case requires precise timing adjustments or manual scene rearrangement, Agent Opus may not be the right fit. Second, visual sourcing is automated but not unlimited. Agent Opus pulls royalty-free images from the web and stock libraries, and you can upload your own assets. However, if your prompt describes highly specific or niche visuals that are not available in these sources, the system will substitute the closest match. For example, if you request 'a photo of our CEO speaking at last year's conference,' you need to upload that image yourself. The email video maker cannot access private or proprietary content unless you provide it. Third, voiceover and avatar generation are high-quality but not perfect replicas. Voice cloning captures tone and cadence well, but subtle inflections or emotional nuance may vary slightly from your natural speech. AI avatars look realistic but are not indistinguishable from real video footage. For most email campaigns, this quality is more than sufficient, but if your brand requires studio-grade production, you may need to supplement with professional recording. Fourth, Agent Opus outputs social-ready video formats optimized for email embeds, landing pages, and platforms like TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. If you need ultra-high-resolution exports for broadcast or cinema, the email video maker is not designed for that. Fifth, the system does not generate captions, subtitles, or transcripts as separate deliverables. It focuses on creating the video itself, with text overlays and motion graphics baked into the visual composition. If you need standalone subtitle files for accessibility compliance, you will need to handle that separately. Finally, Agent Opus is optimized for short-form, campaign-focused video content. If you are creating long-form educational videos, webinars, or documentary-style content, the email video maker may not provide the narrative depth or pacing control you need. It excels at punchy, message-driven videos that fit email and social contexts. Understanding these boundaries helps you use Agent Opus effectively: it is a fast, automated email video maker that replaces manual editing and stock sourcing, not a full-featured video production suite.
