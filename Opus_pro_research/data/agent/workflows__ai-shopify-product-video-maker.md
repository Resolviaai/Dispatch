### How does AI Shopify Product Video Maker handle product descriptions versus full scripts?

AI Shopify Product Video Maker is designed to work with whatever input you have. If you paste a product description from your Shopify listing, the system analyzes the key features, benefits, and selling points, then structures a video narrative around them. It identifies which elements deserve visual emphasis, where to place product shots, and how to pace the reveal of features. The AI automatically expands short descriptions into full video scripts with scene-by-scene breakdowns. If you already have a polished script, the tool respects your structure and focuses on visual execution: matching scenes to script beats, timing transitions, and integrating your product imagery at the right moments. For simple prompts like 'Create a 30-second video highlighting the waterproof feature of this backpack,' Agent Opus generates both the script and the visuals. The key difference is control versus speed. Product descriptions give the AI more creative freedom to structure the narrative. Full scripts give you precise control over messaging while the AI handles all visual production. Both paths result in a complete, publish-ready video. You can also start with a description, review the AI-generated script outline in the preview, and refine your prompt if the emphasis isn't quite right. This flexibility means you're never locked into one workflow. Store owners who write quick bullet points get videos as fast as agencies with full creative briefs.

### What are best practices for prompts when creating Shopify product videos with this tool?

The most effective prompts for AI Shopify Product Video Maker combine three elements: audience, outcome, and key product details. Start by defining who you're targeting. 'Create a video for busy parents' sets a different tone than 'Create a video for tech enthusiasts.' Next, specify the desired outcome: 'that drives add-to-cart clicks' versus 'that builds brand awareness' will produce different pacing and calls-to-action. Finally, highlight the product details that matter most. Instead of 'Make a video for my water bottle,' try 'Create a 20-second Instagram Reel for eco-conscious millennials showcasing the double-wall insulation and sustainable materials of this stainless steel water bottle, ending with a limited-time discount code.' The AI uses audience cues to select visual styles, motion graphics tempo, and voice tone. Outcome goals shape the narrative arc and CTA placement. Product details ensure the right features get screen time. You can also specify format preferences in your prompt: 'vertical video for TikTok' or 'square format for Facebook ads.' If you have brand guidelines, mention them: 'Use upbeat pacing with modern sans-serif text overlays' or 'Keep it minimal and elegant, matching luxury skincare aesthetics.' When uploading product images, name your files descriptively. 'hero-shot.jpg' and 'detail-stitching.jpg' help the AI understand which images to feature prominently versus use as B-roll. For voice, specify energy level: 'enthusiastic and fast-paced' versus 'calm and trustworthy.' The AI adapts. Don't worry about perfect prompts on the first try. You can regenerate with refined instructions, and each iteration teaches you what language produces your ideal output.

### What are the limitations or edge cases when using AI Shopify Product Video Maker for ecommerce?

AI Shopify Product Video Maker excels at creating narrative-driven product videos from text and images, but there are scenarios where results require iteration or additional input. First, the tool works best when you provide high-quality product photography. If your only images are low-resolution or poorly lit, the AI can't enhance image quality beyond basic upscaling. Invest in decent product shots, even if taken on a smartphone with good lighting. The AI handles composition and motion, but it starts with your source material. Second, highly technical or niche products benefit from detailed prompts. If you're selling industrial equipment or specialized software, a generic prompt like 'Make a video for this product' may not capture the nuances that matter to your B2B audience. Provide context: 'Create a video for procurement managers highlighting the ROI of this inventory management system, focusing on time savings and error reduction.' The more specific your prompt, the better the output. Third, the tool generates videos based on the input you provide. If your product description is vague or your script lacks a clear structure, the resulting video will reflect that. Garbage in, garbage out. Take a few minutes to clarify your key selling points before generating. Fourth, while Agent Opus auto-sources royalty-free stock footage and web images when needed, it prioritizes the assets you upload. If you want full control over every visual, upload all the imagery you need rather than relying on auto-sourced content. Fifth, extremely short videos under ten seconds can feel rushed. The AI needs enough time to establish context, showcase features, and deliver a CTA. Aim for 15 to 30 seconds minimum for social ads, longer for product page videos. Finally, the tool is not a replacement for live-action demos that require human interaction or real-world use cases. If your product is best demonstrated by someone using it in a specific environment, consider filming that footage and using it as an uploaded asset. Agent Opus will integrate it into the final video with motion graphics and voiceover, giving you the best of both worlds.
