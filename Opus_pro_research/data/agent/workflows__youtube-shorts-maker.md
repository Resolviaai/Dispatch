### What are best practices for prompts when using a YouTube Shorts maker?

Effective prompts for a YouTube Shorts maker like Agent Opus balance clarity with creative freedom. Start by stating your core message or hook in one sentence, then add context about tone, audience, and desired length. For example, 'Create a 30-second Short explaining why cold showers boost energy, targeting fitness enthusiasts, upbeat tone' gives the system enough direction without over-constraining visual choices. Include specific brand elements if needed: 'Feature our logo in the lower third and use product shots of our water bottle.' Mention visual style preferences like 'modern kinetic text' or 'minimalist aesthetic' to guide motion graphics decisions. If you want a particular voice, specify 'use my cloned voice' or 'choose a friendly female AI narrator.' Avoid overly vague prompts like 'make a video about productivity,' which force the AI to guess your angle. Instead, frame the value: 'Show three quick productivity hacks for remote workers, each under 10 seconds, with bold text overlays.' For blog-to-Short conversions, the URL itself is the prompt, but you can add instructions like 'focus on the top three takeaways' or 'keep it under 45 seconds.' The YouTube Shorts maker interprets natural language, so write as if briefing a video producer. Test different prompt lengths: sometimes a single sentence sparks the best results, other times a short paragraph with scene suggestions yields tighter pacing. After generation, note what worked and refine your next prompt. The system learns your preferences over repeated use, so consistent terminology for tone, pacing, and visual style helps maintain brand coherence across multiple Shorts.

### What are the limitations or edge cases of a YouTube Shorts maker like Agent Opus?

Agent Opus as a YouTube Shorts maker excels at generating complete vertical videos from text, but understanding its boundaries helps you set realistic expectations. First, the system is optimized for Short-form content, typically 15 to 60 seconds. If you need a three-minute explainer, the pacing and visual density may feel rushed, though you can request longer outputs in your prompt. Second, while Agent Opus auto-sources royalty-free stock and web images, highly niche or hyper-specific visuals like 'a red 1987 Porsche 911 on a foggy mountain road at sunrise' may return close approximations rather than exact matches. Uploading your own product shots or custom images solves this. Third, voice cloning requires a clean audio sample of at least 30 seconds; background noise or multiple speakers in the sample will degrade clone quality. Fourth, the YouTube Shorts maker does not provide a manual editing interface. If you want to trim a specific scene by two seconds or swap one image for another, you'll need to regenerate with a revised prompt rather than tweaking a timeline. This trade-off prioritizes speed and automation over granular control. Fifth, AI-generated motion graphics follow modern design trends, but if your brand requires a highly specific animation style like hand-drawn illustrations or 3D renders, you may need to upload pre-made assets. Sixth, the system outputs social-ready 9:16 vertical video by default; if you need square or horizontal formats for other platforms, specify that in your prompt. Finally, while Agent Opus handles most content types well, highly technical or jargon-heavy scripts may benefit from a human review pass to ensure accuracy. These limitations are not roadblocks but guardrails that help you use the YouTube Shorts maker effectively: provide clear prompts, upload key assets, and iterate on outputs to match your exact vision.
