### What are best practices for writing interview scripts that generate high-quality AI videos?

Effective interview scripts for AI video generation follow conversational patterns while providing clear visual cues. Start each question with context that helps the AI select relevant B-roll: instead of 'What's your process?' write 'Walk us through your product development process from idea to launch.' This specificity guides Agent Opus to source imagery related to product design, prototyping, and launch activities. Structure answers in digestible segments with natural pauses, allowing the AI to insert visual transitions and motion graphics at logical breaks. Avoid dense paragraphs; break complex answers into 2-3 sentence chunks that the avatar can deliver with varied pacing and emphasis. Include specific examples, statistics, or case studies in responses because these trigger the AI to generate supporting visuals like charts, product shots, or relevant stock imagery. When writing for an AI interview video maker, think visually: if you mention 'our team grew from 5 to 50 people,' the system will likely generate growth graphics or team imagery. Use descriptive language that suggests visual metaphors: 'building a foundation' might trigger construction or architecture B-roll, while 'navigating challenges' could generate journey or path imagery. For multi-topic interviews, use clear transitional phrases like 'Moving to our next topic' or 'Another key area is' to help the AI recognize segment boundaries and create visual breaks. Specify tone in your prompt: 'energetic startup founder interview' versus 'thoughtful industry expert Q&A' produces different avatar energy and visual pacing. Include 3-5 key points you want visually emphasized, and Agent Opus will add motion graphics or text overlays at those moments. Keep individual answers under 200 words for optimal avatar delivery and visual variety. The AI interview video maker performs best when scripts balance natural conversation flow with visual storytelling opportunities, giving the system clear hooks for dynamic, engaging video assembly.
