### What are best practices for prompts in an automotive video generator for dealership inventory?

Effective prompts for an automotive video generator balance specificity with creative freedom. Start by defining the video's purpose: inventory showcase, lease promotion, service explainer, or customer testimonial overlay. For inventory videos, include the make, model, year, key features, and any special offers. Example: 'Generate a 45-second vertical video showcasing our certified pre-owned 2022 SUV, emphasizing low mileage, all-wheel drive, premium sound system, and our 90-day warranty. Use upbeat music and end with a call-to-action to schedule a test drive.' Agent Opus uses those details to source SUV imagery, create feature callouts with AI motion graphics, and script a voiceover that hits every selling point. For lease promotions, specify the offer terms, target audience, and urgency: 'Create a 30-second promo for our end-of-year lease event, targeting families, highlighting zero-down offers on sedans and SUVs, with a countdown graphic and dealership contact info.' The automotive video generator interprets the urgency and family angle, selecting warm visuals and clear pricing overlays. If you want a specific voice, mention it: 'Use our sales manager's cloned voice' or 'Choose a professional male AI narrator.' For branding consistency, upload your logo, color palette, and any product shots once; Agent Opus applies them automatically. Avoid vague prompts like 'Make a car video.' Instead, describe the outcome: who's watching, what action you want them to take, and which vehicle features matter most. The more context you provide, the more precisely the automotive video generator delivers a video that matches your dealership's voice and campaign objectives.

### What are the limitations or edge cases of an automotive video generator for complex dealership campaigns?

Agent Opus excels at generating polished, single-concept automotive videos from text prompts, but understanding its boundaries helps you plan workflows. First, the automotive video generator does not provide a manual editing interface. You cannot trim scenes, adjust timing, or rearrange clips after generation. If the output doesn't match your vision, refine your prompt and regenerate. This trade-off eliminates editing overhead but requires clear, detailed prompts upfront. Second, while Agent Opus sources royalty-free car imagery and stock footage, it cannot film custom B-roll of your specific lot or showroom unless you upload those shots to your media library. If your campaign demands hyper-local visuals like your exact storefront or a specific vehicle on your lot, provide those images in your prompt or library, and the automotive video generator integrates them. Third, Agent Opus generates videos based on the prompt's scope. If you request a 90-second video covering ten vehicle features, the pacing may feel rushed. For best results, match video length to content density: 30–45 seconds for single-vehicle highlights, 60 seconds for lease promos with multiple models, 90 seconds for educational or testimonial formats. Fourth, the system does not handle interactive elements like clickable hotspots or branching narratives; it produces linear, social-ready videos. Fifth, while Agent Opus supports voice cloning and AI avatars, it does not replace live-action testimonials shot on location. If your campaign requires real customers speaking on camera, film that footage and upload it; Agent Opus can incorporate it into a generated video structure. Finally, the automotive video generator is optimized for social and digital ad formats. If you need broadcast-quality 4K for TV commercials with complex color grading, you may still require a traditional production pipeline. For 95 percent of dealership content, social promos, inventory showcases, service explainers, and digital ads, Agent Opus delivers faster, cheaper, and more scalable results than manual production.
