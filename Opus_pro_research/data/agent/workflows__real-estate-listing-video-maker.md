## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does a real estate listing video maker handle MLS descriptions versus custom scripts?

Agent Opus accepts both raw MLS copy and fully scripted narratives, adapting its scene assembly and pacing to match your input style. When you paste MLS data, the system parses key details like square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, lot size, and special features, then structures a video that highlights those selling points in a logical flow: exterior establishing shot, interior walkthrough by room, amenities and upgrades, neighborhood context, and closing call-to-action. The AI identifies which details deserve visual emphasis and automatically generates motion graphics for price, key stats, and standout features like updated kitchens or pool access. If you provide a custom script with specific language, tone, or storytelling arc, the real estate listing video maker follows your narrative structure beat by beat, matching visuals to your exact wording and pacing voiceover to your intended rhythm. You can also submit a hybrid: MLS facts plus a few sentences of agent commentary or buyer benefits. The system blends factual data with persuasive messaging, ensuring technical accuracy while maintaining emotional appeal. For best results with MLS input, include at least three to five feature bullets and a neighborhood or school district mention so the AI has enough material to build varied scenes. For custom scripts, aim for 150 to 300 words to fill a 60 to 90 second video without rushing or dead air. Both paths produce a complete video with voiceover, motion graphics, and music, no manual assembly required.

### What are best practices for prompts and asset uploads in a real estate listing video maker?

Start with a clear, structured prompt that mirrors how you would verbally describe the property to a buyer. Lead with the headline appeal, such as updated modern farmhouse in top school district or luxury waterfront condo with marina access, then list three to five key features in order of importance: square footage, bed and bath count, standout upgrades like chef's kitchen or spa bath, outdoor spaces, and location benefits like walkability or commute time. The real estate listing video maker uses this hierarchy to prioritize visuals and voiceover pacing. If you upload property photos, name files descriptively: exterior front, kitchen island, master bath, backyard pool. The AI reads filenames to match images to script mentions, so clear labels improve scene accuracy. Aim for eight to twelve photos covering exterior, main living spaces, bedrooms, bathrooms, and any special features. If you rely on auto-sourced stock, specify architectural style and setting in your prompt: mid-century modern in desert landscape or colonial in tree-lined suburb, so the system pulls tonally consistent imagery. For voiceover, decide whether you want a warm, conversational AI voice that appeals to first-time buyers or a confident, authoritative clone of your own voice that reinforces your personal brand. Test both on your first video. Include your brokerage logo as a PNG with transparent background and your headshot if you want an agent intro or outro overlay. Specify any required legal disclaimers or contact details in the prompt so they appear in lower thirds or end cards. Avoid jargon or abbreviations the AI might misinterpret: write three bedrooms instead of 3BR, and spell out HOA or MLS terms. The clearer and more complete your input, the less iteration you need and the faster you go from brief to finished video.

### What are the limitations or edge cases of a real estate listing video maker for complex properties?

A real estate listing video maker excels at standard residential listings but has specific constraints for highly complex or unique properties. Luxury estates with ten-plus rooms, multiple structures, or extensive acreage may require longer videos than a single generation supports, typically capped at two to three minutes. In those cases, generate separate videos for main house, guest house, and grounds, then link them in a playlist or combine them manually if needed. The AI handles up to twelve distinct scenes or rooms well; beyond that, prioritize the most marketable spaces in your prompt and save secondary areas for a follow-up video or photo gallery. Commercial properties with technical specifications, zoning details, or floor plans benefit from a hybrid approach: use the real estate listing video maker for the visual tour and brand intro, then supplement with a PDF or slide deck for legal and technical data the video format cannot convey clearly. Properties under construction or renovation present a challenge if you lack finished photos. Stock imagery can fill gaps, but specify construction stage and style precisely: new build framing in coastal modern style or kitchen mid-renovation with exposed brick, so the AI pulls relevant placeholder visuals. For vacant or unfurnished spaces, the system can source staged interiors from stock libraries, but results vary in realism. If staging is critical to your market, invest in virtual staging images before generating the video. Unique architectural features like atriums, spiral staircases, or custom millwork may not have perfect stock matches; upload your own photos to ensure accuracy. Neighborhood and location context relies on general stock footage, so hyper-local details like a specific park, school building, or downtown corner require you to shoot and upload that B-roll yourself. The real estate listing video maker cannot pull live MLS data or integrate with third-party property databases automatically; you must copy and paste details into the prompt. Finally, legal disclaimers and compliance language must be short and clear. The AI can display text overlays, but dense paragraphs of fine print are better suited to video descriptions or linked documents. For 80 percent of residential listings, these limitations never surface. For the other 20 percent, plan to supplement AI-generated video with manual assets or additional context.
