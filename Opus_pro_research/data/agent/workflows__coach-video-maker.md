### What are best practices for prompts when using a coach video maker?

The quality of your coaching video starts with clarity in your input. When writing a prompt for Agent Opus, lead with the outcome you want your viewer to achieve. Instead of saying 'make a video about goal setting,' try 'create a three minute coaching video that walks a new client through setting a 90 day business goal using the SMART framework.' The more specific you are about structure, tone, and teaching points, the better the AI can match your intent. If you are generating a course module, outline the key concepts in order and note any visuals you want emphasized, like 'show a progress chart when discussing milestones' or 'use calming nature imagery during the mindset section.' For client onboarding videos, describe the journey. 'Welcome new coaching clients, explain the onboarding process in four steps, and end with a calendar booking call to action.' Agent Opus will sequence the scenes to match that flow. If you have brand assets like logos, headshots, or product images, mention them in the prompt so the system prioritizes those visuals over generic stock. For voice, specify the tone. 'Use a warm, conversational coaching voice' or 'clone my voice for consistency across all course videos.' The coach video maker also benefits from examples. If you have a style you love, describe it. 'Generate a video similar to my Instagram Reels, punchy and under 60 seconds, with bold text overlays.' The AI learns from your language and adapts. Finally, iterate. Your first video might be 80 percent there. Tweak the prompt, regenerate, and refine until the output matches your coaching brand perfectly.

### What are the limitations or edge cases when using a coach video maker for complex coaching content?

Agent Opus excels at generating structured coaching videos from text, but understanding its boundaries helps you get the best results. The system works best when your input has a clear narrative or teaching arc. If you paste a 5000 word stream of consciousness brain dump with no structure, the AI will do its best to find a through line, but you will get a better video if you organize your thoughts into sections or bullet points first. For highly technical coaching content that relies on detailed diagrams, live software demos, or whiteboard style drawing, Agent Opus can incorporate static images of those elements if you provide them, but it does not generate interactive diagrams or screen recordings. If your coaching method depends on showing a specific tool interface or drawing concepts in real time, you may need to create those assets separately and reference them in your prompt so the system includes them as visual anchors. Another edge case is videos that require precise timing or cuts synced to music beats. Agent Opus handles pacing and background soundtracks well for standard coaching content, but if you need frame perfect sync for a high energy promo or a music driven motivational piece, you might need a more manual approach. The coach video maker also does not handle multi speaker formats natively. If your coaching video requires a conversation between you and a guest or client, you will need to script that as a single narration or provide separate audio files. Finally, while Agent Opus supports voice cloning and avatars, the avatar is best suited for talking head style delivery. If your coaching brand relies on dynamic on camera movement, gestures, or live interaction with props, the AI avatar may feel static. In those cases, consider using Agent Opus for the bulk of your content library and reserving live recording for signature pieces. The key is to match the tool to the content type. For explainer videos, course modules, client onboarding, social proof clips, and educational content, the coach video maker handles the full workflow. For edge cases that require live interaction or highly custom visuals, use Agent Opus to generate the foundation and layer in custom elements where needed.
