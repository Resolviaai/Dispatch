### What are best practices for prompts in a conference video maker workflow?

The quality of your conference video starts with the clarity of your input. Agent Opus works best when you provide structured, outcome-focused prompts. For a keynote recap, start with the speaker's name, their title, the session theme, and the top three takeaways. For example: 'Create a 60-second recap of Dr. Sarah Chen's keynote on AI ethics. Highlight her three principles: transparency, accountability, and inclusivity. Use our conference logo and a professional tone.' For panel discussions, list each speaker, their perspective, and the central debate or question. Include any memorable quotes or data points you want featured. For sponsor content, specify the sponsor's value proposition, the call to action, and any required branding elements like logos or product shots. If you're working from a script, paste it directly—Agent Opus will parse speaker cues, timing notes, and visual directions. If you're starting from an event blog post or session summary, drop in the URL and let Agent Opus extract the narrative structure. The more context you provide about your audience and distribution channel, the better. Mention if the video is for LinkedIn (professional, data-driven), Instagram (fast-paced, visual), or an internal event recap (detailed, inclusive). Specify the desired length: 30 seconds for a teaser, 60-90 seconds for a recap, or 2-3 minutes for a deep dive. If you have brand guidelines—color palettes, font styles, logo placement rules—reference them in your prompt or upload a brand kit. Agent Opus will apply those constraints automatically. Avoid vague prompts like 'make a video about the conference.' Instead, be specific: 'Create a 90-second highlight reel of our SaaS Summit, featuring three customer success stories, our CEO's opening remarks, and a call to action for next year's event.' The conference video maker thrives on detail, so treat your prompt like a creative brief. The clearer your input, the faster you'll get a publish-ready video.

### What are the limitations or edge cases of a conference video maker like Agent Opus?

Agent Opus is designed for speed and automation, which means it's optimized for text-driven conference video creation—not live event capture or multi-camera editing. If you need to edit raw footage from a conference (trimming speaker segments, syncing multiple camera angles, color-grading stage lighting), Agent Opus isn't the right tool. It's built for generating net-new videos from text inputs: session summaries, speaker bios, event recaps, sponsor messages. If you already have recorded conference footage and want to repurpose it, you'll need a traditional video editor. Agent Opus shines when you're creating promotional teasers before the event, recap videos after sessions, or sponsor content that doesn't rely on live footage. Another edge case: highly technical or niche conference topics. If your event covers specialized fields—quantum computing, advanced biomedical research, legal compliance—you'll want to review the AI-generated script and visuals carefully. Agent Opus sources imagery from the web and stock libraries, so it may not always find domain-specific visuals (e.g., a specific lab setup or a proprietary software interface). In those cases, upload your own images or product shots to ensure accuracy. The voiceover system is excellent for clear, professional narration, but it won't replicate the spontaneous energy of a live speaker. If your conference brand relies on charismatic, off-the-cuff delivery, consider using the avatar feature with your own recorded voice rather than relying solely on AI voiceover. For very long conference videos—10+ minutes—Agent Opus can generate them, but you'll get better results by breaking the content into shorter segments (keynote recap, panel discussion, sponsor spotlight) and publishing them as a series. This also aligns with social media best practices, where shorter videos perform better. Finally, Agent Opus doesn't handle real-time collaboration or live streaming. If you need to generate a conference video during a live event and publish it within minutes, you'll need to work fast: write the prompt, upload any last-minute assets, and generate. The tool is fast, but it's not instantaneous. For most conference video use cases—pre-event promos, post-session recaps, sponsor content, speaker highlights—Agent Opus is the fastest, most consistent solution available. Just know its strengths (text-to-video automation, brand consistency, zero editing) and its boundaries (not a live footage editor, not a real-time streaming tool).
