### What are best practices for prompts when creating save-the-date or ceremony highlight videos?

Effective wedding video maker prompts balance storytelling structure with specific visual and emotional cues. Start by defining the video's purpose and audience. A save-the-date video needs different pacing and tone than a ceremony recap or vendor showcase. For save-the-dates, focus your prompt on the couple's story, wedding date and location, and invitation to guests. Include romantic details like how you met, proposal highlights, or what makes your venue special. For ceremony highlights, structure your prompt chronologically: processional, vows, ring exchange, first kiss, recessional, reception moments. Specify emotional beats you want emphasized, like a tearful parent moment or joyful celebration scene. Use sensory language to guide visual selection. Instead of 'outdoor wedding,' write 'sunset garden ceremony with rose arbor and string lights.' Instead of 'happy couple,' describe 'bride laughing in lace gown, groom in navy suit, golden hour lighting.' Include audio preferences: do you want romantic instrumental background music, upbeat celebration tracks, or a specific mood? Specify if you want voiceover narration telling your story or pure visual storytelling with text overlays. For vendor portfolios, describe your signature style and the specific wedding's unique elements. Mention color palettes, decor themes, and standout details that showcase your work. Keep prompts focused but detailed, typically 150 to 300 words for a complete wedding video. If you have a ceremony script or timeline, paste it directly. Agent Opus will extract key moments and structure scenes accordingly. Test different prompt styles to see what visual results resonate best with your aesthetic preferences.
