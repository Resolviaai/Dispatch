## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the q&a video generator work end to end?

You describe what you want — a topic, a mood, a script outline, or just a vibe — and Agent Opus assembles a full Q&A video from it. The agent handles every part of production: scene composition, B-roll selection, motion graphics, captions, voiceover, beat-synced transitions, and platform-specific resizing. The signature look — question-bubble overlays, talking-head framing, animated text reveals, and conversational pacing — is baked into the generation by default, so the output reads as on-aesthetic from the first frame without you having to art-direct every shot. You can override or customize any element in the prompt: change the color palette, adjust pacing, swap the music style, or specify on-screen text. The system runs frequency analysis on any uploaded audio to time visual intensity to the music, and pacing analysis on uploaded scripts to match scene cuts to natural breaks. Most projects go from idea to finished export in under five minutes, with three aspect ratios (9:16, 1:1, 16:9) rendered simultaneously.

### What prompts produce the best q&a video results?

Effective Q&A video prompts combine three things: the topic, the mood, and the specific visual language you want featured. Lead with what the video is about (a recipe, a tour, a tutorial, a tribute), name the emotional tone (energetic, contemplative, intimate, playful), and reference concrete visual elements ("question-bubble overlays," "soft natural light," "hand-lettered captions"). The more specific the visual references, the more precisely the first generation lands. If your content has clear sections — intro, main, outro, or chapter breaks — describe how visuals should shift between them so the agent paces transitions correctly. Avoid vague phrases like "make it cool" or "something interesting"; the model treats those as low-signal and falls back to generic styling. You can also reference real creators, films, or moodboards as anchors and the agent interprets the aesthetic without copying directly. Iterate by regenerating with refinements — usually two or three passes get you to a final cut.
