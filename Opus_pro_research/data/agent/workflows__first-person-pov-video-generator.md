You describe what you want — a topic, a mood, a script outline, or just a vibe — and Agent Opus assembles a full first-person POV video from it. ### What prompts produce the best first-person pov video results?

Effective first-person POV video prompts combine three things: the topic, the mood, and the specific visual language you want featured. Lead with what the video is about (a recipe, a tour, a tutorial, a tribute), name the emotional tone (energetic, contemplative, intimate, playful), and reference concrete visual elements ("head-mounted camera angle," "soft natural light," "hand-lettered captions"). The more specific the visual references, the more precisely the first generation lands. If your content has clear sections — intro, main, outro, or chapter breaks — describe how visuals should shift between them so the agent paces transitions correctly. Avoid vague phrases like "make it cool" or "something interesting"; the model treats those as low-signal and falls back to generic styling. You can also reference real creators, films, or moodboards as anchors and the agent interprets the aesthetic without copying directly. Iterate by regenerating with refinements — usually two or three passes get you to a final cut.
