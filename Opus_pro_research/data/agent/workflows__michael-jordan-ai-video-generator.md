### How does a Michael Jordan AI video generator handle different content types like motivational clips versus product demos?

Agent Opus adapts its scene assembly and pacing based on your input. The system will source basketball imagery—courts, hoops, action shots—and layer AI motion graphics that emphasize intensity and movement. It will pace cuts to match the rhythm of your voiceover, creating a highlight-reel feel even if you're not using actual game footage. For product demos, you upload your product shots and brand assets, then describe how you want the Michael Jordan energy applied—maybe a sneaker launch with fast cuts, dynamic zooms, and motivational narration. Agent Opus integrates your assets into the visual flow, using basketball-inspired motion graphics to frame your product as part of a winning story. The key difference is input: motivational content relies more on AI-sourced imagery and script-driven pacing, while product demos require you to supply specific brand visuals. Both workflows use the same one-prompt system, but the output style shifts based on what you describe and what assets you provide. You can also mix approaches—start with a motivational hook, transition to product features, and close with a call to action—all in a single prompt.

### What are best practices for writing prompts in a Michael Jordan AI video generator to get the most compelling basketball-inspired visuals?

Start with clear intent: are you creating a motivational speech, a brand story, a training tip, or a product launch? Specify the emotional tone—inspirational, aggressive, triumphant, reflective—and reference visual cues like 'highlight reel energy,' 'slow-motion intensity,' or 'fast-paced cuts.' For example, instead of 'make a video about basketball,' write 'create a 30-second motivational video with Michael Jordan-style intensity, featuring fast cuts of basketball courts, close-ups of sneakers, and a voiceover about overcoming failure.' The more specific you are about pacing, visual style, and narrative arc, the better Agent Opus can assemble scenes. If you're using brand assets, describe where they should appear: 'open with our logo over a basketball court, transition to product shots with dynamic zooms, close with a call to action over a slow-motion dunk sequence.' You can also reference Michael Jordan's career themes—competition, resilience, legacy—to guide the AI's sourcing and motion graphics. For voiceover, specify tone: 'confident and direct,' 'reflective and inspiring,' or 'energetic and urgent.' If you're cloning your voice, record a sample that matches the energy you want. For scripts, break them into clear beats: hook, problem, solution, call to action. Agent Opus will pace visuals to match each beat. Finally, test and iterate—your first prompt might be broad, but you can refine based on the output, adjusting pacing, visual density, or narrative focus in follow-up prompts.

### What are the limitations or edge cases of a Michael Jordan AI video generator when it comes to licensed footage or specific player imagery?

Agent Opus does not provide licensed NBA footage, official Michael Jordan game clips, or trademarked imagery. It sources royalty-free basketball visuals—generic courts, hoops, action shots, training sequences—and uses AI motion graphics to create a Michael Jordan-inspired aesthetic. This means you're not getting actual footage of Michael Jordan dunking in the 1998 Finals; you're getting basketball imagery styled to evoke that energy and legacy. If you need official Michael Jordan content, you would need to license it separately and upload it as a custom asset, which Agent Opus can then integrate into your video. For brand and product content, this limitation is rarely an issue—most creators want the vibe and storytelling style, not literal game footage. However, if you're producing content that requires specific player likenesses, team logos, or official NBA branding, you'll need to secure those rights independently. Agent Opus can incorporate them once you provide them, but it won't source them automatically. Another edge case: if your prompt is too vague—'make a Michael Jordan video'—the output may lack focus. The system works best with clear narrative direction, so specify the story, tone, and visual style. Finally, voice cloning requires a clean audio sample; background noise or inconsistent tone can affect quality. For avatars, if you're using your own face, lighting and camera quality in your source footage matter. Agent Opus is powerful, but it's not magic—it amplifies good inputs and clear direction.
