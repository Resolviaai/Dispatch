### How do I write effective prompts for a doctor office video maker?

Effective prompts for the doctor office video maker start with clear communication goals and specific visual direction. Begin by stating the video's purpose explicitly, such as 'Create a 60-second patient education video explaining Type 2 diabetes management' or 'Generate a new patient welcome video for a family medicine practice.' This framing helps Agent Opus understand the context and tone. Next, provide either a complete script with specific language you want in the voiceover, or give a detailed outline of key points to cover. For medical content, being specific about terminology helps, for example, 'Explain the difference between urgent care and emergency room visits, emphasizing when patients should choose each option.' Include visual guidance by describing scenes you envision, such as 'Show a family in a waiting room, then transition to a doctor consultation, then display our office exterior.' The doctor office video maker responds well to structured prompts that break content into sections, like 'Introduction: Welcome message from Dr. Smith. Section 1: Our approach to preventive care. Section 2: Services we offer. Conclusion: How to schedule an appointment.' Specify the tone you want, whether 'warm and reassuring for anxious patients' or 'professional and informative for insurance updates.' If you have brand elements, mention them: 'Include our practice logo and use our blue and green color scheme.' For voice, indicate whether you want 'a calm, authoritative female voice' or 'use my uploaded voice clone.' Length matters, so specify 'create a 30-second Instagram Reel' or 'generate a 2-minute YouTube video.' The more context you provide about your practice type, patient demographic, and communication style, the better Agent Opus tailors the output. You can also reference existing content by providing a blog post URL and instructing 'turn this article about flu prevention into a 45-second video for TikTok.' Best practices include starting with shorter videos to test your prompt style, then scaling to longer content once you see how the system interprets your instructions. Avoid vague prompts like 'make a doctor video' and instead be specific: 'Create a 90-second video explaining our new telehealth platform, showing how patients download the app, schedule virtual visits, and connect with providers, using friendly, accessible language for older adults unfamiliar with technology.'
