### How does an event recap video maker handle different input formats like briefs versus full scripts?

Agent Opus accepts four input types for event recap video generation: a short prompt or brief, a detailed script, a structured outline, or a blog or article URL. Each format gives the AI different levels of creative control. A brief might say 'Three-day SaaS conference in Austin, keynote on AI automation, 200 attendees, highlight sponsor booths and networking sessions.' The system interprets that high-level description, decides scene order, selects visuals that match conference energy, and writes connective voiceover to tie moments together. A full script gives you sentence-by-sentence control over narration and scene cues, so the AI follows your exact pacing and messaging while still handling all visual sourcing, motion graphics, and audio mixing. An outline sits in between: you list key segments like 'Opening remarks, Panel discussion, Product demo, Closing Q&A,' and the AI expands each segment into scenes with appropriate visuals and voiceover transitions. A blog URL is ideal when you have a written event recap already published; paste the link and the system extracts structure, quotes, and key moments to build a video narrative. Regardless of input type, the event recap video maker outputs a complete, publish-ready file with no manual editing required. The best practice is to match input detail to your creative confidence: use a brief when you trust the AI to interpret event tone and pacing, use a script when you need exact messaging control, and use an outline when you want structured guidance without writing every word.

### What are best practices for writing prompts that produce high-quality event recap videos?

Effective prompts for an event recap video maker balance specificity with creative freedom. Start with event fundamentals: type of event, location or setting, audience size, and primary goal. For example, 'Annual product launch event, virtual webinar format, 500 registrants, goal is to showcase three new features and customer testimonials.' Next, list must-include moments or themes: 'Highlight CEO opening remarks, feature demo of dashboard redesign, include two customer quotes, close with call to action for free trial.' The AI uses these anchors to structure scenes and prioritize visuals. Then describe tone and energy: 'Professional but approachable, upbeat pacing, modern tech aesthetic.' This guides motion graphics style, soundtrack selection, and voiceover delivery. If you have brand assets, mention them: 'Use company logo in lower third, integrate product screenshots from slide deck, apply brand color palette to transitions.' The system will source those assets or pull royalty-free alternatives that match your visual identity. Avoid vague instructions like 'Make it exciting' or 'Keep it short'; instead say 'Target 60 seconds, fast cuts between speakers, emphasize innovation theme.' The event recap video maker interprets concrete details far better than abstract adjectives. If your event had multiple tracks or sessions, prioritize which moments matter most: 'Focus on keynote and breakout session two, skip exhibitor hall footage.' Finally, specify output format if you have a primary platform: 'Optimize for LinkedIn feed, 16:9 aspect ratio, include captions for sound-off viewing.' The AI adjusts pacing and text overlays accordingly. The golden rule: treat your prompt like a creative brief you would give a video editor, but skip technical instructions about timelines or rendering because the system handles all production mechanics automatically.

### What are the limitations or edge cases when using an event recap video maker for complex or multi-day events?

While an event recap video maker like Agent Opus handles most event formats effectively, certain scenarios require thoughtful input design to achieve the best results. Multi-day conferences with dozens of sessions pose a content prioritization challenge: the AI cannot watch hours of footage and decide which moments matter most, so your prompt must explicitly list priority segments. For example, 'Day one keynote, day two product demo, day three closing panel' gives clear structure, whereas 'Summarize three-day conference' forces the system to guess. If your event includes live audience reactions, spontaneous moments, or unscripted interactions, the AI cannot generate those organically because it works from text inputs, not raw video footage. In those cases, describe the moment in your script: 'Show audience applauding after demo, cut to attendee testimonial quote' and the system will source appropriate reaction visuals or use stock imagery that conveys the intended emotion. Another edge case is highly technical or niche events where industry-specific jargon, acronyms, or visual metaphors are essential. The AI performs best when you define terms in your prompt or script: 'Explain API integration using simple animation of data flowing between systems' rather than assuming the system knows your product architecture. For events with multiple speakers or panelists, the AI can generate avatar-based delivery or voiceover narration, but it cannot replicate the exact appearance or mannerisms of real speakers unless you upload custom avatar footage. If speaker authenticity is critical, consider using voiceover narration that references speakers by name rather than attempting to recreate their on-camera presence. Timing is another consideration: if your event recap needs to publish within hours of the live event ending, ensure your input is ready immediately. The event recap video maker generates videos in minutes, but drafting a clear prompt or script still requires human time. Finally, very short events like 15-minute webinars may not provide enough content substance for a compelling recap; in those cases, consider generating a promotional teaser or highlight reel instead of a full recap format. The system excels when you give it structured input, clear priorities, and realistic expectations about what text-to-video AI can synthesize versus what requires human creative judgment.
