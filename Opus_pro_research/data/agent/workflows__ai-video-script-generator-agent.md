### How does Agent Opus turn an AI video script into a finished video?

Agent Opus uses a multi-stage AI pipeline to transform your script into a complete video. First, it analyzes your script's structure, identifying key narrative beats, product mentions, and emotional tone. Then it generates a scene plan, determining how many scenes are needed, their duration, and the visual style for each. Next, the system sources or generates visuals: it can pull royalty-free stock footage, search the web for relevant images, or use your uploaded brand assets like logos and product shots. AI motion graphics are applied to create dynamic transitions, text overlays, and visual effects that match the script's pacing. Simultaneously, Agent Opus generates or clones voiceover based on your preferences, syncing speech to the script's timing. If you choose an avatar, the system animates it to match the voiceover's cadence and emotional tone. Finally, Agent Opus composites all elements, adds a background soundtrack, and exports the video in social-ready aspect ratios. The entire process is automated, so you never open a timeline or manually adjust cuts. You simply input your AI video script, configure voice and style preferences, and receive a publish-ready video. This approach eliminates the traditional bottleneck of manual editing, allowing creators and marketers to scale video production without expanding their team or learning complex software. The system is designed for speed and consistency, ensuring that every video generated from your script maintains professional quality and brand alignment.

### What are best practices for writing an AI video script for Agent Opus?

To get the best results from Agent Opus, structure your AI video script with clear narrative beats and visual cues. Start with a strong hook in the first 10 seconds: a question, bold statement, or problem that grabs attention. This helps Agent Opus prioritize dynamic visuals and motion graphics at the open. Break your script into distinct sections or scenes, using paragraph breaks or numbered points to signal transitions. This guides the AI's scene assembly and pacing logic. Be explicit about key visuals you want: if you mention a product feature, name it directly so Agent Opus knows to source or display that asset. For example, instead of 'our solution helps teams,' write 'our dashboard shows real-time analytics.' This specificity improves visual matching. Include tone and style notes if your script has emotional shifts, such as 'urgent,' 'celebratory,' or 'educational.' Agent Opus uses these cues to adjust motion graphics intensity and voiceover delivery. Keep sentences concise and conversational, especially if you're using AI voiceover. Long, complex sentences can sound unnatural when synthesized, so aim for a rhythm that mirrors how you'd speak on camera. If you're uploading brand assets, reference them in the script: 'show logo here' or 'insert product shot.' This ensures Agent Opus places them at the right moments. Finally, test different script lengths. Shorter scripts (30 to 60 seconds) work best for social platforms like TikTok and Reels, while longer scripts (2 to 3 minutes) suit YouTube explainers or tutorials. Agent Opus adapts pacing and scene count based on script length, so align your writing with your distribution goal.

### What are the limitations or edge cases of generating videos from an AI video script?

Agent Opus excels at turning structured AI video scripts into finished videos, but there are scenarios where the system's automation has boundaries. First, highly abstract or poetic scripts can challenge the visual matching logic. If your script uses metaphorical language without concrete nouns, Agent Opus may default to generic stock footage or motion graphics. To avoid this, include explicit visual cues or descriptive phrases that guide the AI's sourcing. Second, if your script references very niche or proprietary visuals that aren't available in stock libraries or web searches, you'll need to upload those assets manually. Agent Opus can't generate custom 3D models or illustrations from scratch, so if your product requires specific technical diagrams or unique animations, provide those files upfront. Third, voiceover quality depends on script clarity. If your script has complex jargon, acronyms, or non-standard pronunciation, AI voices may stumble. Voice cloning mitigates this, but even cloned voices need clear, conversational scripts to sound natural. Fourth, Agent Opus generates videos in standard social aspect ratios and durations, but it doesn't support interactive elements like clickable buttons, embedded forms, or branching narratives. If your use case requires those features, you'll need to add them in post-production outside Agent Opus. Fifth, the system is optimized for single-speaker narratives. If your AI video script involves multiple characters or dialogue, Agent Opus can handle it, but you'll need to specify voice changes or avatar switches in the script itself. Finally, while Agent Opus sources royalty-free and stock content, it's your responsibility to verify licensing if you're using the video for commercial purposes in regulated industries. The system provides attribution metadata, but final compliance checks are on the user. Understanding these edge cases helps you write AI video scripts that play to Agent Opus's strengths, ensuring smooth generation and minimal manual intervention.
