### What are best practices for prompts and scripts when creating fitness explainer videos?

Effective fitness explainer video prompts balance instructional detail with creative flexibility. Start by defining your teaching objective clearly. Instead of a vague prompt like create a squat video, write create a 90-second explainer video teaching proper barbell back squat form for beginners, covering stance width, bar placement, descent depth, and knee tracking, with voiceover in an encouraging coaching tone. This gives the fitness explainer video maker specific content to visualize and a clear instructional arc. Structure longer scripts using phases that mirror how you would teach in person. For a workout tutorial, organize by warm-up, main exercises, and cool-down. For technique breakdowns, use setup, execution, and common errors. Agent Opus uses these structural cues to pace visuals and transition between scenes logically. Include sensory and anatomical details that translate well to motion graphics. Phrases like feel the stretch in your hamstrings, keep your core braced, or drive through your heels give the system clear visual concepts to illustrate with overlays, arrows, or highlighted muscle groups. Specify any branding or product integration upfront. Mention your gym logo, supplement product, or app interface in the prompt so Agent Opus incorporates these assets naturally rather than adding them as an afterthought. For voiceover tone, describe the emotional quality you want. Motivational and energetic works for HIIT content, while calm and instructional suits yoga or rehabilitation videos. If you are cloning your voice, the fitness explainer video maker preserves your natural coaching cadence, but the script still guides pacing and emphasis. When creating series content, maintain consistent terminology and visual metaphors across videos. If you use a specific cue like hinge at the hips in one video, use the same language in related content so Agent Opus builds a cohesive visual vocabulary for your brand. Finally, test short-form content first. A 60-second exercise demo lets you refine your prompting style before investing time in longer educational series.
