### How does script to video handle different script formats and lengths?

Agent Opus accepts scripts in multiple formats and adapts its video generation to match your input structure. You can paste a traditional screenplay with scene headings and dialogue, a simple bullet-point outline, a narrative blog post, or even a two-sentence brief. The AI parses your script to identify key scenes, talking points, and pacing cues. For longer scripts, Agent Opus automatically segments content into logical scenes and applies appropriate transitions. For shorter scripts, it expands visual coverage by generating complementary motion graphics and B-roll sequences that reinforce your message. The system recognizes dialogue vs narration, action vs description, and adjusts voiceover delivery and visual pacing accordingly. If your script includes specific visual directions like "show product demo" or "cut to testimonial," Agent Opus interprets those cues and sources matching visuals or generates appropriate graphics. You can also include timing notes or emphasis markers, and the AI will adjust scene duration and voiceover inflection. The script to video engine is built to handle real-world writing styles, so you don't need to reformat your existing scripts or learn special syntax. Whether you're working from a polished final draft or a rough first pass, Agent Opus generates a complete video that reflects your script's intent and structure.

### What are best practices for writing scripts that generate the best videos?

To maximize the quality of your script to video output, focus on clear, concrete language and explicit visual cues. Start each major section or scene with a brief description of what should appear on screen. For example, instead of writing "We offer great customer service," write "Show customer support team responding to chat messages on laptop screens." This gives Agent Opus specific visual targets to source or generate. Use active verbs and avoid abstract concepts without visual anchors. Break longer scripts into distinct scenes or segments, ideally 15 to 45 seconds each, to help the AI maintain pacing and visual variety. If you want specific branding elements to appear, call them out explicitly: "Display logo in corner" or "Show product packaging on white background." For voiceover scripts, write in a conversational tone that sounds natural when spoken aloud. Read your script out loud before generating to catch awkward phrasing or run-on sentences. Include pauses or emphasis markers where you want the voiceover to slow down or stress a word. If your script includes data, statistics, or lists, format them clearly so Agent Opus can generate matching on-screen text or animated charts. Avoid overly long sentences; aim for 15 to 20 words per sentence for optimal voiceover pacing. Finally, specify any mood or tone preferences at the top of your script, such as "energetic and upbeat" or "calm and professional," so the AI can select appropriate music, motion graphics speed, and visual style. These practices help Agent Opus generate videos that align closely with your creative vision while minimizing the need for revisions.

### How does script to video handle voiceover timing and scene synchronization?

Agent Opus automatically synchronizes voiceover with visuals by analyzing your script's structure and pacing. When you generate a video, the AI first processes your script to estimate speaking duration based on word count, sentence complexity, and natural speech patterns. It then segments your script into scenes and assigns appropriate visual coverage to each segment. The voiceover is generated or cloned to match the estimated timing, and the AI adjusts scene duration to align with the audio. If a particular sentence or paragraph requires more visual emphasis, Agent Opus extends the scene duration and adds B-roll, motion graphics, or animated text to fill the time without dead air. Conversely, if a section is brief, the AI tightens the visual pacing and uses faster transitions. You can also include explicit timing cues in your script, such as "pause for 2 seconds" or "hold on this visual for 5 seconds," and Agent Opus will honor those directions. The script to video engine uses natural language processing to detect emphasis, questions, and emotional shifts in your script, then adjusts voiceover inflection and visual pacing to match. For example, a question in your script might trigger a visual pause or a zoom-in effect to create anticipation before the answer. If your script includes dialogue between multiple speakers, Agent Opus can assign different voices or avatar appearances to each speaker and cut between them naturally. The synchronization happens automatically during generation, so you don't need to manually align audio and video tracks. If you want to fine-tune timing after generation, you can regenerate with adjusted script phrasing or add explicit timing notes, and Agent Opus will produce a new version with updated synchronization. This automated timing and sync capability is what makes script to video generation so much faster than traditional editing workflows.
