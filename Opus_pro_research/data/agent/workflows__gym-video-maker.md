### What are best practices for prompts when using a gym video maker?

Effective gym video maker prompts balance specificity with creative freedom. Start with the video's goal: 'Generate a 60-second Instagram Reel demonstrating beginner kettlebell swings' or 'Create a 90-second YouTube intro for our new spin class.' Then add structural details: 'Open with a hook question, show three form cues with on-screen text, close with a membership CTA.' Agent Opus uses this framework to pace scenes and select visuals. Include sensory language to guide tone: 'high-energy cuts with upbeat soundtrack' for a bootcamp promo versus 'calm, steady shots with minimal text' for a yoga intro. If you want specific exercises featured, list them: 'Show burpees, mountain climbers, and jump squats in 10-second intervals.' For branding, reference assets by name: 'Use our gym logo in the lower third and trainer headshot in the intro.' When creating educational content, structure your prompt like a lesson plan: 'Explain the mind-muscle connection in bench press: setup, breathing, tempo, and peak contraction. Use side-angle footage and slow-motion on the eccentric phase.' For promotional gym videos, lead with the offer: 'Announce our January membership special: no enrollment fee, first month half off. Show group classes, personal training, and locker room amenities. End with QR code and website URL.' Agent Opus interprets these cues and assembles a video that matches your intent. Avoid vague prompts like 'Make a gym video'—the more context you provide about audience, platform, and desired outcome, the more precisely the gym video maker delivers. If your first result needs adjustment, refine your prompt with specific feedback: 'Add more close-ups of proper grip' or 'Slow down the transition between exercises.'

### How does a gym video maker handle technical fitness terminology and exercise cues?

Agent Opus interprets fitness-specific language and translates it into clear visual storytelling. When you write 'Demonstrate scapular retraction during the row's concentric phase,' the gym video maker identifies the key concept—shoulder blade movement—and selects footage or generates graphics that highlight that motion. On-screen text appears at the moment of retraction, and voiceover explains the cue in plain language. For complex movements like the Olympic clean, break your prompt into phases: 'Show the setup, first pull, transition, catch, and recovery. Use slow-motion on the transition and freeze-frame the catch position.' Agent Opus structures the video to match this progression, ensuring viewers see each phase distinctly. If you include coaching cues—'Keep the bar close to the body' or 'Drive through the heels'—the system overlays these as text callouts synchronized with the relevant footage. For anatomy-focused content, describe what you want shown: 'Highlight the quadriceps during the squat's eccentric phase using a muscle overlay graphic.' The gym video maker sources or generates visuals that illustrate muscle engagement, making abstract concepts concrete. When creating videos for different skill levels, adjust your prompt's technical depth: 'Beginner deadlift video: focus on hip hinge and neutral spine with simple language' versus 'Advanced deadlift video: discuss bar path optimization, leverages, and sticking points with biomechanical terms.' Agent Opus adapts vocabulary and visual complexity to match. For certification prep or trainer education, submit detailed scripts with precise terminology, and the system preserves accuracy while adding visual aids. If you're producing a video series on periodization, progressive overload, or program design, the gym video maker maintains terminological consistency across episodes, so 'hypertrophy' or 'rate of perceived exertion' is explained the same way every time. This technical fluency means you don't need to dumb down your content or hire a specialist editor—Agent Opus handles fitness language as naturally as it handles general marketing copy.
