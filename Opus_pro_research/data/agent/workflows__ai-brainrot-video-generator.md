### What are the limitations or edge cases when generating brainrot videos with AI?

Agent Opus handles most brainrot concepts effectively, but understanding its boundaries helps you craft better prompts and set realistic expectations. The system excels at visual density and rapid pacing but has practical limits on layer complexity. Extremely intricate prompts requesting ten simultaneous visual elements may result in cluttered output where individual components become illegible, especially on mobile screens. In these cases, simplifying your prompt or breaking the concept into multiple videos produces better results. Timing precision is another consideration. While Agent Opus matches voiceover to visuals automatically, very specific timing requirements like "meme insert at exactly 7.3 seconds" are harder to guarantee than natural timing cues like "meme reaction after each key point." The AI also works within platform constraints, so requesting 10-minute brainrot videos will be chunked into shorter segments since the aesthetic loses effectiveness beyond 90 seconds. Content sourcing has guardrails too. Agent Opus uses royalty-free and licensed stock footage, so requests for specific copyrighted gameplay or branded content may be fulfilled with similar alternatives rather than exact matches. If you need specific game footage or meme clips, providing them as custom assets ensures accuracy. Voice cloning requires a clean audio sample of at least 30 seconds for optimal results. Shorter samples work but may have slight quality variations. The system also assumes you want social-ready vertical video by default. If you need landscape or square formats, specify that in your prompt. Finally, while Agent Opus generates original content, extremely niche internet subcultures or very recent meme formats may not be recognized if they emerged after the AI's training cutoff. In those cases, describing the visual concept rather than naming the meme helps the system understand your intent.
