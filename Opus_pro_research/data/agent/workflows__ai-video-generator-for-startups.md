### How does an AI video generator for startups handle different input types like prompts, scripts, and blog posts?

Agent Opus accepts four input formats, each optimized for different startup workflows. First, a short prompt works when you need speed. Describe your video concept in a few sentences, like 'product demo for our new mobile checkout feature, highlight speed and security, 30 seconds for Instagram,' and the AI generates scenes, voiceover, and visuals based on that brief. Second, a full script gives you precise control over narration. Paste your product launch script, and Agent Opus matches visuals and pacing to each line, building a structured video that follows your exact messaging. Third, an outline format bridges the two. Provide bullet points or a rough structure, and the AI expands them into a complete video with natural pacing and transitions. Fourth, a blog or article URL lets you repurpose written content. Agent Opus reads the post, extracts key points, and assembles a video summary with motion graphics and voiceover, turning SEO content into social video without rewriting. All four methods produce the same output quality: finished, publish-ready videos with AI motion graphics, branded visuals, and professional voiceover. Startups often begin with prompts for speed, then move to scripts as their messaging matures. The flexibility means one tool supports every stage of a startup's content evolution, from MVP launch videos to polished investor decks.

### What are best practices for writing prompts when using an AI video generator for startups?

Effective prompts for Agent Opus balance specificity with creative freedom. Start with the core message: what is the one thing viewers must understand? For a SaaS startup, that might be 'our tool automates invoice reconciliation for accounting teams.' Next, define the format and platform: '30-second Instagram Reel' or '60-second LinkedIn explainer.' This guides pacing and aspect ratio. Then add stylistic cues: 'energetic tone,' 'professional and trustworthy,' or 'founder-led and conversational.' These shape voiceover delivery and visual tempo. Include any must-have visuals: 'show our dashboard UI,' 'feature our logo prominently,' or 'use product screenshots from the app store.' Agent Opus will source or integrate these assets. If you have brand colors or a specific aesthetic, mention it: 'use blue and white color scheme' or 'modern, minimalist motion graphics.' Avoid over-prescribing shot-by-shot details; the AI excels at scene assembly when given outcomes rather than instructions. For example, instead of 'start with a zoom into the dashboard, then cut to a user clicking a button,' write 'demonstrate how users complete a task in three clicks.' The AI will visualize that flow with appropriate motion graphics and transitions. Startups with tight messaging can paste a one-liner pitch and let Agent Opus expand it into a full video. Teams with detailed scripts can input the full text and trust the AI to match visuals and pacing. The best practice is to treat the prompt like a creative brief: clear on goals, flexible on execution.

### Can an AI video generator for startups maintain consistent branding across multiple videos?

Yes, and brand consistency is critical for startups building recognition. Agent Opus supports custom asset uploads, so you can provide your logo, brand colors, product screenshots, and any proprietary visuals once, then reuse them across every video. When you upload a logo, the AI places it in consistent positions, typically lower thirds or end cards, matching professional video standards. Product screenshots and UI mockups integrate directly into scenes, so demos and feature announcements show real interfaces rather than generic stock imagery. Voice consistency comes through two paths: voice cloning and AI voice selection. Voice cloning captures a founder's or team member's voice from a short sample, then uses that voice for all narration. This is powerful for startups where the founder's voice is part of the brand identity. Alternatively, select one AI voice and use it across all videos to create a recognizable audio signature. Avatar options work the same way. Upload footage of a team member once, and Agent Opus can use that person as the on-camera presenter in future videos, or choose a consistent AI avatar that becomes the face of your product updates. Visual style consistency comes from the AI's motion graphics engine, which applies uniform animation styles, transitions, and text treatments across projects. Startups often create a 'template' by generating one video with their desired look, then referencing that style in future prompts: 'use the same motion graphics style as our last product launch video.' Agent Opus learns from your preferences and maintains visual coherence. The result is a library of videos that look and sound like they came from the same production team, even though each was generated independently from text.

### What are the limitations or edge cases when using an AI video generator for startups?

Agent Opus excels at structured, message-driven video content, but certain scenarios require awareness of its design boundaries. First, highly abstract or artistic concepts may not translate perfectly from text prompts. If your startup's brand relies on surreal visuals or experimental cinematography, the AI's motion graphics and scene assembly are optimized for clarity and communication rather than avant-garde aesthetics. You can still achieve polished, modern results, but expect a professional-commercial look rather than fine-art film. Second, real-time or live-action footage of specific people or locations must be provided as uploads. Agent Opus cannot generate original live-action video of your office, your team, or a specific event. It can, however, integrate uploaded clips into the final video alongside AI-generated graphics and stock visuals. Third, very long-form content, like 10-minute product deep-dives, may require breaking the project into segments. The AI is optimized for social and web video lengths (15 seconds to 3 minutes), where pacing and viewer attention are tightly managed. For longer content, generate multiple videos and sequence them, or provide a detailed script with clear section breaks. Fourth, niche or highly technical jargon may need clarification in prompts. If your startup operates in a specialized field like biotech or quantum computing, add brief explanations in your script so the AI can source appropriate visuals and avoid generic metaphors. Fifth, real-time collaboration or multi-user editing is not part of the workflow. Agent Opus is a generation tool, not a collaborative editing platform. One person inputs the prompt or script, and the system outputs a finished video. If your team needs to review and iterate, plan for a review-and-regenerate cycle rather than simultaneous editing. Finally, while Agent Opus auto-sources royalty-free and stock visuals, highly specific or proprietary imagery must be uploaded. If your product demo requires exact UI states or branded environments, provide those assets in advance. Understanding these boundaries helps startups set realistic expectations and structure their video workflows for maximum efficiency.
