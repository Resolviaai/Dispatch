### How does a case study video maker handle different story formats and data types?

A case study video maker like Agent Opus is designed to work with the full spectrum of customer success content, from short testimonial quotes to multi-page case study documents. You can input a brief bullet list of results, a full narrative script, an outline with key metrics, or even paste a URL to an existing blog-based case study. The AI analyzes the structure and identifies critical elements like the customer challenge, your solution, implementation steps, and measurable outcomes. It then organizes these into a logical video narrative with distinct scenes for problem setup, solution introduction, results reveal, and testimonial or call-to-action close. For data-heavy case studies with charts, percentages, or timelines, the motion graphics engine automatically visualizes metrics as animated overlays, comparison slides, or progress bars. If your case study includes customer quotes, the AI can present them as text overlays, voiceover narration, or both. The system also adapts tone and pacing based on content length: a 200-word brief becomes a punchy 30-second highlight reel, while a 1,500-word case study expands into a 90-second deep dive with multiple chapters. You maintain full control by adjusting the prompt to emphasize specific outcomes, add context about industry or use case, or request a particular visual style. This flexibility means one case study video maker can serve B2B SaaS teams showcasing ROI, e-commerce brands highlighting customer transformations, agencies presenting campaign results, and consultants building thought leadership, all without requiring separate tools or workflows for each format.

### What are best practices for prompts when using a case study video maker?

Effective prompts for a case study video maker start with clarity about the customer and the outcome. Begin by naming the customer or customer type, the challenge they faced, and the measurable result your solution delivered. For example, instead of a vague prompt like 'make a case study video,' try 'create a 60-second case study video showing how Acme Corp reduced support tickets by 40% in three months using our AI chatbot.' This gives the AI concrete anchors for scene structure and visual emphasis. Next, specify any brand assets or visuals you want included. Mention if you have a customer logo, product screenshots, before-and-after images, or team photos. The AI will integrate these into the video automatically, but calling them out in the prompt ensures they appear in the right scenes. If your case study has standout metrics like revenue growth, time savings, or user adoption rates, list them explicitly and ask the AI to highlight them with motion graphics. For example, 'emphasize the 3x ROI and 25% faster onboarding with animated charts.' Tone and style matter too. Indicate whether you want a formal, data-driven presentation or a warm, testimonial-focused story. Phrases like 'professional B2B tone' or 'friendly, customer-first narrative' guide the AI's language and pacing choices. If you have a specific platform in mind, mention it. Saying 'optimized for LinkedIn' will produce a square or vertical video with text overlays sized for mobile, while 'YouTube case study' might yield a longer, landscape format with chapter markers. Finally, if you want voiceover or an avatar, specify that upfront. You can request your cloned voice, an AI voice with a particular accent or gender, or an on-camera avatar to present the case study as a talking-head video. The more context you provide about audience, platform, and desired emphasis, the more precisely the case study video maker will match your vision on the first generation.

### What are the limitations or edge cases of a case study video maker for complex customer stories?

A case study video maker excels at transforming structured customer success stories into video, but there are scenarios where the tool's automation has natural boundaries. First, highly technical or niche case studies with deep domain jargon may require additional context in your prompt to ensure the AI interprets terms correctly. For example, if your case study involves specialized software, medical devices, or financial instruments, providing a brief explanation of key terms or acronyms helps the AI generate accurate narration and visuals. Without that context, the system might default to generic descriptions or miss the nuance that makes the case study compelling to your target audience. Second, case studies that rely heavily on proprietary data visualizations or custom infographics may need you to upload those assets directly. While the AI can generate motion graphics for standard metrics like percentages, timelines, and comparisons, it cannot recreate a complex, multi-variable dashboard or a branded chart design from scratch. In these cases, you would upload your existing graphic as an image, and the AI will integrate it into the video with transitions and overlays. Third, case studies with multiple customer voices or interview clips present a challenge for fully automated generation. If your case study includes quotes from three different stakeholders, the AI can present them as text overlays or narrate them in a single voice, but it cannot generate distinct voice clones for each person unless you provide separate audio samples. For video testimonials or on-camera interviews, you would need to upload those clips, and the AI can weave them into the generated video alongside motion graphics and B-roll. Fourth, very long case studies over 2,000 words may require you to either condense the content or split it into multiple videos. While the AI can handle extended narratives, video length impacts engagement, and most platforms favor shorter formats. The tool will prompt you to prioritize key sections if the input is too dense for a single video. Finally, case studies that depend on live-action footage, such as factory tours, event coverage, or hands-on product demos, fall outside the scope of text-to-video generation. The AI cannot create original live-action scenes, but you can upload that footage and use the case study video maker to add branded intros, motion graphics for results, voiceover narration, and a polished outro. Understanding these boundaries helps you prepare the right inputs and set realistic expectations, ensuring the case study video maker delivers maximum value for your specific use case.
