## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does an AI scene generator handle different input types like prompts versus full scripts?

Agent Opus adapts its scene generation process based on the input you provide. When you submit a short prompt or brief, the AI expands your idea into a structured narrative, then builds scenes that match the tone and pacing you describe. It infers visual style, motion graphics complexity, and scene transitions from context clues in your prompt. For example, a prompt like 'explain our SaaS pricing in 60 seconds' triggers a fast-paced, text-heavy scene structure with animated graphics and clear voiceover pacing. If you provide a full script, Agent Opus treats each paragraph or dialogue block as a distinct scene beat. It parses your script for natural scene breaks, matches visuals to the narrative arc, and synchronizes voiceover timing to your written pacing. The AI also respects formatting cues like scene headings or speaker labels if you include them. When you paste a blog URL or article, the system extracts key points, condenses the content into a video-friendly structure, and generates scenes that mirror the article's logical flow. Each scene corresponds to a section or argument in the original text, with visuals sourced to match the topic. Regardless of input type, the AI scene generator maintains consistent visual style, voiceover tone, and motion graphics complexity across all scenes in the final video. You can mix input types too. Start with a brief to generate a draft, then refine specific scenes by adding script details in a follow-up prompt. The system merges your edits into the existing scene structure without rebuilding from scratch. This flexibility lets you control how much creative direction you provide while still getting a complete, publish-ready video.

### What are best practices for writing prompts that produce high-quality scenes with an AI scene generator?

Effective prompts for an AI scene generator balance specificity with creative freedom. Start by defining your video's purpose and audience in one sentence. For example, 'Create a 90-second product demo for B2B marketers highlighting our analytics dashboard.' This gives the AI context for tone, pacing, and visual style. Next, outline the key points or story beats you want covered. You don't need a full script, just the main ideas in order. Something like 'Scene 1: problem statement, Scene 2: dashboard walkthrough, Scene 3: results and CTA' helps the AI structure the narrative and allocate time per scene. Specify visual preferences when they matter. If you want your product UI featured prominently, say 'show dashboard screenshots in Scene 2.' If you prefer abstract motion graphics over literal visuals, note 'use animated icons and data visualizations instead of stock footage.' The AI scene generator will prioritize your visual direction while filling gaps with appropriate assets. Describe the voiceover tone and pacing. Phrases like 'energetic and fast-paced' or 'calm and educational' guide the AI's narration style and scene timing. If you're cloning your voice, mention whether you want a conversational or formal delivery. Include brand elements in your prompt. Reference your logo placement, brand colors, or specific product shots you've uploaded. The AI will integrate these assets consistently across scenes. Avoid over-specifying technical details like exact frame counts or transition types. The AI handles those decisions based on best practices for the platform and format you're targeting. Instead, focus on the message, audience, and desired outcome. Test iterative prompts. Generate a draft, review the scenes, then refine with a follow-up prompt like 'make Scene 2 more visual and less text-heavy' or 'add a customer testimonial in Scene 3.' The AI scene generator adapts without rebuilding the entire video.

### What are the limitations or edge cases when using an AI scene generator for complex video projects?

Agent Opus handles most video generation tasks end-to-end, but understanding its boundaries helps you plan projects effectively. The AI scene generator excels at narrative-driven videos with clear structure, like explainers, product demos, tutorials, and social content. It struggles with highly abstract or experimental visual concepts that lack clear narrative logic. If your vision requires surreal imagery or non-linear storytelling, you may need to provide more detailed visual direction or accept that the AI will interpret your prompt literally. Scene length and complexity have practical limits. The system generates videos up to several minutes long, but extremely long-form content like 20-minute webinars may require breaking the project into segments. Each segment can be generated separately with consistent branding, then combined if needed. Very short scenes under three seconds can feel rushed because the AI needs time to establish visuals and sync voiceover. If you need rapid-fire cuts, consider grouping related ideas into slightly longer scenes. The AI sources royalty-free and stock assets automatically, but highly specific or niche visuals may not always be available. If you need footage of a rare product, unique location, or proprietary process, upload those assets yourself. The AI scene generator will prioritize your uploads over generic stock footage. Voice cloning works best with clear, high-quality audio samples. Background noise, heavy accents, or inconsistent recording conditions can affect clone accuracy. If your cloned voice sounds off, re-record your sample in a quiet space with consistent microphone distance. AI avatars are effective for talking-head scenes but don't replicate complex human expressions or body language. If your video requires nuanced emotional delivery or physical demonstrations, consider using your own video avatar or focusing on voiceover with motion graphics instead. The system generates social-ready outputs, but platform-specific features like interactive polls, clickable links, or live captions require post-generation additions through the platform itself. Agent Opus delivers the video file ready to upload, not platform-native interactive elements. Brand asset integration assumes you've uploaded the files. If you reference a logo or product shot in your prompt but haven't uploaded it, the AI will generate a placeholder or skip that element. Always upload key assets before generating scenes that depend on them.
