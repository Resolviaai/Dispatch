### How does an animated explainer video maker like Agent Opus handle different input formats?

Agent Opus accepts four input types for animated explainer video creation: a short prompt or brief, a full script, a structured outline, or a blog or article URL. Each format triggers a different generation path. When you provide a brief, Agent Opus expands it into a narrative structure, determines scene count and pacing, and generates the script internally before assembling visuals and motion graphics. If you paste a complete script, the system parses dialogue, identifies key concepts, and maps each segment to a visual scene with corresponding animations and transitions. Outline mode is ideal when you have bullet points or a rough structure but want Agent Opus to fill in connective tissue and voiceover copy. Blog URL input extracts the article text, condenses it into explainer-friendly language, and builds a video that summarizes or illustrates the written content. Across all formats, Agent Opus applies the same motion graphics engine, sourcing relevant stock images or web visuals, layering kinetic text, and synchronizing voiceover timing. You can upload brand assets like logos or product screenshots regardless of input type, and the system integrates them into the final animation. This flexibility means you can start from any stage of your content workflow, whether you have a polished script ready or just a rough idea, and still get a finished animated explainer video without manual scene assembly or editing.

### What makes AI motion graphics in an animated explainer video maker different from template-based tools?

Traditional explainer video tools rely on pre-built templates where you swap text and images into fixed layouts. Agent Opus uses AI motion graphics that generate animations dynamically based on your content and context. Instead of choosing a template and filling placeholders, you describe what you want or provide a script, and the system composes scenes from scratch. The AI analyzes your narrative to determine which concepts need visual emphasis, then applies kinetic text animations, shape morphing, icon transitions, and layered compositing to match the pacing and tone of your message. For example, if your script introduces a problem and then a solution, Agent Opus might animate a split-screen transition with contrasting visuals, or use a reveal effect to highlight the solution moment. These decisions happen automatically, informed by the semantic meaning of your script rather than a one-size-fits-all template. The motion graphics engine also adapts to the assets you upload. If you provide a product screenshot, Agent Opus can animate it with zoom, pan, or highlight overlays to draw attention to specific features. If you include a logo, the system positions and animates it as a branded element within the scene flow. This approach means every animated explainer video is unique to your content, not constrained by template boundaries. You get professional-grade motion design without needing After Effects skills or a motion designer on staff, and the output feels custom-built rather than cookie-cutter.

### What are the best practices for writing prompts or scripts for an animated explainer video maker?

Effective prompts and scripts for Agent Opus balance clarity, structure, and visual cues. Start by defining your core message in one sentence, then expand into a three-act structure: problem, solution, and call to action. For prompts, be specific about the audience, tone, and key points you want to cover. Instead of a vague brief like create an explainer video about our app, write create a 60-second animated explainer video for small business owners explaining how our invoicing app saves time by automating payment reminders and tracking overdue invoices, with a friendly, professional tone. This gives Agent Opus enough context to generate a focused narrative and select appropriate visuals and motion graphics. When writing a full script, use short sentences and active voice. Explainer videos thrive on concise, punchy language that translates well to voiceover. Avoid jargon unless your audience expects it, and break complex ideas into bite-sized chunks. Include visual cues in your script by describing what should appear on screen. For example, instead of just writing our platform integrates with your existing tools, add show icons of popular tools like Slack, Google Drive, and Salesforce appearing on screen. Agent Opus interprets these cues and sources or generates the corresponding visuals. Pacing matters. Aim for roughly 150 words per minute of video, which gives the voiceover room to breathe and the motion graphics time to register. If your script runs long, Agent Opus may compress scenes or speed up transitions, which can reduce impact. Structure your script with clear transitions between sections. Use phrases like but here is the problem or here is how it works to signal narrative shifts, and Agent Opus will apply visual transitions like wipes, fades, or split-screens to reinforce those moments. Finally, specify any brand elements you want included. Mention your logo placement, product screenshots, or specific colors in the prompt or script notes, and upload the corresponding assets so Agent Opus can integrate them seamlessly into the animation.
